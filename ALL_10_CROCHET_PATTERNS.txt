PATTERN 1 - DESIGN CODE NS 01 - HAMISH THE HIGHLAND COW
================================================================
US crochet terms - Intermediate - 15 cm / 6 in sitting - 6-8 hours

A shaggy, sturdy Highland cow with a flattened brow, a wide cream muzzle and a
weather-beaten fringe. Worked in pieces and assembled, so every proportion can
be pinned and checked before it is sewn.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SAFETY -- READ THIS FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------------

Hamish has two 12 mm safety eyes. Safety eyes are small parts. Lock the
washers firmly from the inside and pull-test each one on the finished toy -- a
properly locked washer will not come off by hand. This pattern locks them after
the muzzle is sewn, which is the right order: you cannot re-position an eye once
the washer is on.

The horns, ears, muzzle, legs and tail are all sewn on, and the fringe and
tail are knotted yarn. Sew every seam twice, weave every end in for at least
5 cm, and knot the fringe and tail securely. Loose strands are the first thing a
child will pull.

This pattern has not been tested to a toy-safety standard such as ASTM F963
or EN 71, so please do not describe finished Hamishes as "baby-safe" or "suitable
from birth" in your listings. For children under three, embroider the eyes
with black floss instead of using safety eyes.


----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Yarn A -- Ginger. Worsted / aran (#4). The whole cow uses about 25 g;
one 80 g / 150 m ball covers it several times over, which is what you want if
you plan to make more than one or to re-do the fringe. Caramel, toffee or ginger
gold.

Yarn B -- Oat cream. Worsted / aran (#4), about 12 g. Muzzle, horns,
inner ears, optional belly patch.

Yarn C -- Dark chocolate. Worsted / aran (#4), about 5 g. Hooves and
nostrils.

Yarn D -- Rust. Optional, about 6 g for the scarf, or 28 cm of 12 mm
tartan ribbon.

Hook -- 3.5 mm (US E-4). Smaller than the ball band so stuffing cannot show.

Eyes -- 2 x 12 mm black safety eyes, or embroider for under-threes.

Stuffing -- Polyester fiber fill, about 30 g. Hamish holds roughly
405 cm³; at a firm pack that is 22-32 g. The head and body alone take about
20 g packed firmly. Buying 50 g gives you room to over-stuff the base, which
is what makes him sit.

Notions -- Stitch marker, tapestry needle, pins, scissors.


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE & FINISHED SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Gauge -- 11 sc x 12 rounds = 5 cm / 2 in in Yarn A (4.55 mm per stitch,
4.17 mm per round). Not critical, but loose tension lets stuffing show -- drop
half a millimetre if your fabric is gappy.

Finished size -- About 15 cm / 6 in sitting in the worsted version, with a
70 mm wide body and a 70 x 67 mm head.


----------------------------------------------------------------

ABBREVIATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

MR magic ring - ch chain - sl st slip stitch - sc single crochet
- hdc half double crochet - inc 2 sc in one stitch - dec invisible
decrease - BLO back loop only - FO fasten off - (n) stitch count

Work in a continuous spiral unless a round says join. Mark the first stitch of
every round. Leave 30-40 cm tails on every piece you will sew.


----------------------------------------------------------------

CORE TECHNIQUES
----------------------------------------------------------------

These are the techniques Hamish uses, in the order you will meet them.

1 - THE MAGIC RING. Wrap the yarn once around two fingers, insert the hook,
pull up a loop and work the first round into the ring, then pull the tail to
close it. Pull the tail tight once the first round is complete.

2 - WORK IN A SPIRAL. Do not join and do not ch 1 between rounds. Keep a
stitch marker in the first stitch of every round and move it up each time --
there is no join to count from.

3 - THE INVISIBLE DECREASE. Insert the hook through the front loops only
of the next two stitches, yarn over and pull through both, then yarn over and
pull through the remaining two loops. Every dec in this pattern is worked this
way; it leaves no ridge on the visible rounds.

4 - BACK LOOP ONLY (BLO). Work into the far loop of the stitch only, leaving
the near loop unused. The unused loops form a raised ridge. Hamish's hoof line
is one BLO round.

5 - WORKING AROUND A CHAIN (the oval). For the belly patch you crochet into
both sides of a starting chain: work across the front of the chain, put the
corner increases into the last chain, then continue back along the opposite side
and finish into the last remaining loop. That is what turns a chain into a flat
oval instead of a strip.

6 - THE LARK'S HEAD KNOT. Fold a strand in half, push the folded loop under
a stitch with your hook, then pull the two loose ends through the loop and
cinch. Used for every fringe strand and for the tail.

7 - LADDER STITCH. The invisible seam. Bring the needle out on one side,
pick up one stitch bar on the opposite side, then one bar back on the first
side, alternating, and pull snug every few stitches. The head-to-body join needs
two full passes.


----------------------------------------------------------------

1. HEAD - YARN A - MAKE 1
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked from the top of the head down toward the neck. One straight round only --
this is what keeps the brow flat and wide instead of egg-shaped.

RND     INSTRUCTION            STS   NOTE
------  ---------------------  ----  -----------------------------------
Rnd 1   6 sc in MR             (6)
Rnd 2   inc in each st around  (12)
Rnd 3   [sc, inc] x6           (18)
Rnd 4   [2 sc, inc] x6         (24)
Rnd 5   [3 sc, inc] x6         (30)
Rnd 6   [4 sc, inc] x6         (36)  fringe row 3
Rnd 7   [5 sc, inc] x6         (42)  fringe row 2 / horns
Rnd 8   [6 sc, inc] x6         (48)  fringe row 1 / ears
Rnd 9   sc in each st around   (48)  eyes at Rnd 9-10
Rnd 10  [6 sc, dec] x6         (42)
Rnd 11  [5 sc, dec] x6         (36)
Rnd 12  [4 sc, dec] x6         (30)  stuff firmly, flatten front to back
Rnd 13  [3 sc, dec] x6         (24)
Rnd 14  [2 sc, dec] x6         (18)
Rnd 15  [sc, dec] x6           (12)  last pinch of stuffing
Rnd 16  dec x6                 (6)

Finish. FO. Close the hole with the tail.

Eyes. Place the 12 mm safety eyes between Rnds 9 and 10, 7 stitches apart
and a touch low on the face. Seven stitches is about 32 mm, just under half the
head width. Leave the washers off until the muzzle is pinned.

Finished head measures about 70 mm wide x 67 mm tall -- a flattened sphere at
0.96 height-to-width, which is the Highland silhouette.


----------------------------------------------------------------

2. MUZZLE - YARN B - MAKE 1
----------------------------------------------------------------

RND    INSTRUCTION            STS   NOTE
-----  ---------------------  ----  -------------------------
Rnd 1  6 sc in MR             (6)
Rnd 2  inc in each st around  (12)
Rnd 3  [sc, inc] x6           (18)
Rnd 4  [2 sc, inc] x6         (24)
Rnd 5  sc in each st around   (24)  face at full width, 35 mm
Rnd 6  sc in each st around   (24)
Rnd 7  sc in each st around   (24)
Rnd 8  [2 sc, dec] x6         (18)  rim = 26 mm across

Finish. FO, 40 cm tail. Stuff lightly.

Pinning. The muzzle face is about 35 mm across -- that is the wide cream
patch you see. The rim it is sewn by is about 26 mm, which on this gauge
spans roughly six rounds of head.

Pin the top edge just under the eyes at Rnd 10 and let the lower edge fall
at Rnd 15-16, near the chin. Center it and let the face sit a little wider
than the eye spacing. Sew with small whip stitches, adding a whisper more
stuffing before you close.

Nostrils (Yarn C). Two short vertical satin stitches, 3 stitches apart, on
the lower third. Mouth. One tiny horizontal stitch or a shallow V below
them. Lock the safety-eye washers now.


----------------------------------------------------------------

Rnd 10  sc in each st around   (48)  body at full width, 70 mm
Rnd 11  sc in each st around   (48)
Rnd 12  sc in each st around   (48)

Rnd 14  [6 sc, dec] x6         (42)

Body height is now 19 rounds ~ 79 mm, and the head adds about 67 mm. With the

----------------------------------------------------------------

4. BELLY PATCH - YARN B - OPTIONAL
----------------------------------------------------------------

A two-tone belly. Skip it for a plain ginger front -- both are correct.

----------------------------------------------------------------

5. LEGS - MAKE 4 - START YARN C
----------------------------------------------------------------

All four legs are identical; the sitting pose comes from where and at what angle
you sew them. Sixteen rounds gives a 67 mm leg.

RND     INSTRUCTION            STS   NOTE
------  ---------------------  ----  --------------------------------------
Rnd 1   6 sc in MR             (6)   Yarn C
Rnd 2   inc in each st around  (12)
Rnd 3   [sc, inc] x6           (18)
Rnd 4   sc in each st around   (18)
Rnd 5   sc in each st around   (18)
Rnd 6   BLO sc around          (18)  change to Yarn A -- ridge = color line
Rnd 7   sc in each st around   (18)
Rnd 8   [sc, dec] x6           (12)  stuff hoof firmly
Rnd 9   sc in each st around   (12)
Rnd 10  sc in each st around   (12)
Rnd 11  sc in each st around   (12)
Rnd 12  sc in each st around   (12)
Rnd 13  sc in each st around   (12)  upper leg light
Rnd 14  [2 sc, dec] x3         (9)
Rnd 15  sc in each st around   (9)
Rnd 16  sc in each st around   (9)

Finish. FO, 30 cm tail. Leave the top open.

Hoof ridge. The BLO round and the color change happen on the same round
(Rnd 6), so the ridge lands exactly on the cream-to-ginger line and reads as the
rim of the hoof.

Leg length -- read this before you sew. The legs are 67 mm long, and the
joins are much lower than that: the front join at Rnd 8-9 is about 33-37 mm
above the body base, and the back join at Rnd 3-6 is only 12-25 mm above it. A
67 mm leg therefore has to splay a long way from straight down before its hoof
lands on the surface -- about 55° from vertical at the front and about
68-70° at the back, which is close to horizontal and out to the side. That
is the low, sprawling Highland sit.

If you would rather have a tidier, more upright sit with the hooves just in
front of the chest, work the front pair only to Rnd 10 and stop there
(42 mm). At that length they reach the surface at a natural 20-25° angle. Leave
the back pair at 16 rounds as haunches. Pin all four before you sew anything.


----------------------------------------------------------------

----------------------------------------------------------------

Both layers reach 12 stitches so Rnd 4 can be worked through both.

Rnd 6  [2 sc, dec] x3        (9)
Rnd 7  sc in each st around  (9)

Place the inner on the outer, cream side facing you, before Rnd 4. Flatten, FO,
and pinch the base with 2-3 stitches so the ear cups forward.


----------------------------------------------------------------

7. HORNS - MAKE 2 - YARN B
----------------------------------------------------------------

RND    INSTRUCTION           STS
-----  --------------------  ---
Rnd 1  4 sc in MR            (4)
Rnd 2  [sc, inc] x2          (6)
Rnd 3  sc in each st around  (6)
Rnd 4  sc in each st around  (6)
Rnd 5  [2 sc, inc] x2        (8)
Rnd 6  sc in each st around  (8)
Rnd 7  sc in each st around  (8)

Short and slightly curved -- tilt each one out and a little back when you sew.


----------------------------------------------------------------

8. TAIL - YARN A
----------------------------------------------------------------

Cut 6 strands of Yarn A, each 22 cm / 8.5 in. Fold the bundle in half, pull the
folded loop through a stitch at the center-back just above the last increase
round of the body, and pull the tails through the loop (lark's head). Folding
the bundle gives you 12 hanging ends; divide them into 3 groups of 4 and braid
for 4 cm. Knot firmly and trim into a small tassel.


----------------------------------------------------------------

9. THE FRINGE - YARN A
----------------------------------------------------------------

Attach after the horns and ears are sewn so you can part the hair around them.

Cut 44 strands of Yarn A, each 14 cm / 5.5 in. For depth, swap 6 of them for
Yarn C. Attach with a lark's head knot: fold a strand in half, insert the hook
under a stitch, grab the folded loop, pull it through, then pull both tails
through the loop and cinch.

Work a horseshoe from one ear, across the brow, to the other ear, filling three
rows on Rnds 8, 7 and 6 of the head: every stitch on Rnd 8 (the front row), then
every other stitch on Rnds 7 and 6. Across a half-round horseshoe that is about
24 + 10 + 9 = 43 knots, which is what the 44 strands are for.

Tousle with your fingers. Trim so the fringe grazes the eyes and breaks into
uneven points. Highland hair is weather-beaten, not a salon fringe -- if it looks
tidy, it is not finished.


----------------------------------------------------------------

10. OPTIONAL TARTAN SCARF - YARN D
----------------------------------------------------------------

Ch 61. Row 1: hdc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across (60).
Row 2: ch 1, turn, hdc across (60). FO. Add a 3-strand tassel at each end,
or weave a second color in a slip-stitch stripe to hint at tartan. Tie loosely
under the muzzle.

Ribbon shortcut: 28 cm of 12 mm rust tartan ribbon, knotted once.


----------------------------------------------------------------

ASSEMBLY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Pin everything before you sew. Photograph the pinned piece -- it is the best
pattern note you will ever make.

1 - Muzzle & face. Already done if you followed section 2.

2 - Horns. Sew between Rnds 5 and 7, 6 stitches apart, angled out and
slightly back.

3 - Ears. Sew just outside and a little below each horn, centered on
Rnds 8-9, cupped forward.

4 - Fringe. Attach and trim as in section 9.

5 - Head to body. Sit the closed head on the open neck (Rnd 19 of the body,
an 18-stitch ring about 26 mm across). Because the head is fully closed this is
a ball-on-ring join with no flat seat, and the ring only grips the head about
8 mm up from its base -- so it carries real weight on a shallow purchase.
Ladder-stitch all the way around twice, pack the neck firmly before you
start, and tilt the muzzle a few degrees down as if Hamish is listening.

6 - Back legs. Sew to the lower sides of the body across Rnds 3-6, almost
on the base, splayed out to the side. From a join this low a 67 mm leg needs
about 68-70° from vertical before the hoof is level with the base, so they end
up close to horizontal -- that is what makes him sit.

7 - Front legs. Sew to the FRONT of the body across Rnds 8-9, about 8
stitches apart, angled forward and down -- about 55° from vertical, per the note
in section 5. Joined any higher, the hooves dangle.

8 - Belly patch. Only if you made it.

9 - Tail - 10 - Scarf. Weave in every end and squeeze to settle the
stuffing. A shot of cool air from a hairdryer will fluff the fringe.


----------------------------------------------------------------

THREE SIZES, ONE PATTERN
----------------------------------------------------------------

VERSION        YARN                 HOOK     EYES      HEIGHT  YARN A  STUFFING
-------------  -------------------  -------  --------  ------  ------  --------
Wee Hamish     DK / #3              2.75 mm  8-9 mm    ~12 cm  50 g    ~15 g
Classic        Worsted / #4         3.5 mm   12 mm     ~15 cm  80 g    ~30 g
Cuddle Hamish  Bulky chenille / #5  5.0 mm   16-18 mm  ~21 cm  160 g   ~80 g

Stitch counts stay the same. Only the yarn, hook and stuffing change. Because
the stitch count is fixed, yarn scales with the square of the height and
stuffing with the cube -- so the Cuddle version needs far more of both than
it first appears, and chenille covers fewer metres per gram than worsted on top
of that. Buy generously for the big one.


----------------------------------------------------------------

TROUBLESHOOTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

He will not sit. Restuff the body base more firmly and sew the back legs
lower and wider -- closer to horizontal than you think.

Front hooves float. They are sewn too high. They belong on Rnds 8-9 of the
body, not under the neck. If they still float, the legs are simply too long for
the join: work the front pair to Rnd 10 (section 5).

Stuffing shows. Drop half a hook size, or hold a matching thread with the
yarn on visible rounds.

Face looks blank. The eyes are too high. Low eyes plus a wide muzzle is the
Highland stare.

Fringe is sparse. Add a fourth row behind the horns, and mix in one darker
shade.

Horns flop. Under-stuffed at the tip, or sewn only at the edge. Stitch a
full round into the head fabric.

Head tips forward. The neck ring is small relative to the head and only
grips it about 8 mm up. Pack the neck before closing and make the second
ladder-stitch pass tight.


----------------------------------------------------------------

THE FINISHED PROJECT
----------------------------------------------------------------

All pieces before assembly. Lay every component out and check it against
the pattern before you sew anything: 1 head, 1 muzzle, 1 body, 4 legs, 2 inner
and 2 outer ears, 2 horns, a tail and a fringe -- plus the belly patch and scarf
if you are making them.

Finished project. Hamish sitting, with the front hooves flat on the surface.

PATTERN 2 - DESIGN CODE NS 02 - KAWAII HALLOWEEN MINI SET
================================================================
Boo the Ghost - Pip the Pumpkin - Bramble the Bat

US crochet terms - Intermediate - 2.0-2.2 in / 5-5.5 cm each

Three pocket-sized Halloween minis: a ruffled bell ghost, a deeply sculpted squat
pumpkin and a picot-winged bat. Small, fast and forgiving -- but the picots, the
worked-in-the-round-then-flat wing, and the needle sculpting on Pip put this above
a first project.


----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Yarn -- DK / light worsted (#3) cotton.
Cream about 8 g (Boo) - pastel orange about 10 g (Pip) - lavender about 9 g
(Bramble) - sage green about 4 g (stem, tendril and leaf) - pale pink about 2 g
(ear linings).

These are working amounts, not measured consumption. The set itself uses well
under 5 g of each color; the figures allow for tails, sewing, swatching and a
second attempt. One small ball of each color is more than enough.

Hook -- 2.5 mm (US C-2). Tight enough that stuffing cannot show.

Safety eyes -- 2 pairs of 6 mm: one pair for Boo, one pair for Bramble. Pip is
a sculpted pumpkin with an embroidered face and needs none.

Floss -- Black for Boo's smile and Pip's face, pink for blush, white for
Bramble's fangs.

Stuffing -- Polyester fiber fill. Pip takes about 2 g, Bramble about 1 g.
Boo takes none -- he is an open bell that stands on his hem. About 5 g covers
the set with margin.

Notions -- Tapestry needle, stitch markers, pins, scissors.

    Safety. Boo and Bramble use safety eyes, which are a small part and a
    choking hazard. These minis are decorative pieces, not toys for young children,
    and they have not been tested to a toy-safety standard such as ASTM F963 or
    EN 71. If you want to give them to a child, embroider the faces instead of using
    safety eyes and check every seam first.


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE
----------------------------------------------------------------

DK cotton on a 2.5 mm hook: about 3.5 mm per stitch and 3.2 mm per round.

Check it on Pip after Rnd 6 -- 36 stitches should measure about 40 mm around a
firmly stuffed body. Every measurement in this pattern follows from that gauge.


----------------------------------------------------------------

FINISHED SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------

         width                                                         height
-------  ------------------------------------------------------------  -------------------------------
Boo      27 mm at the body, flaring to about 40 mm at the ruffled hem  48 mm including the hem
Pip      40 mm                                                         42 mm
Bramble  33 mm                                                         51 mm, plus an 11.5 cm wingspan

Note on Boo: his width and height are measured on different parts of him. The
27 mm figure is the body tube; the ruffled hem flares well past it. If you quote
one number, quote the hem.


----------------------------------------------------------------

ABBREVIATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

MR         magic ring
---------  -----------------------------------------
ch         chain
sl st      slip stitch
sc         single crochet
hdc        half double crochet
dc         double crochet
inc        increase -- 2 sc in one stitch
dec        invisible decrease

[n]        the stitch count at the end of that round


----------------------------------------------------------------

WORKING NOTES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Work the bodies in continuous spiral rounds -- do not join and do not chain 1
between rounds. Move a stitch marker into the first stitch of every round.

Bramble's wings are the exception: they are worked flat, in turned rows.

Unless a note says otherwise, work every stitch through both loops.


----------------------------------------------------------------

HOW IT COMES TOGETHER
----------------------------------------------------------------

The four techniques this set depends on, in the order you meet them.

1 - The picot

Chain 3, then slip stitch into the 3rd chain from the hook. That makes one small
pointed bump. Picots are used on Bramble's wing edges -- they are what make the
wings read as bat wings rather than leaves.

Keep your picot tension even. A loose picot loops; an over-tight one curls under
and disappears.

2 - Working into one loop only

Boo's ruffled hem is worked into the front loops of Rnd 12, which leaves the
back loops free as a clean ring to sew the optional base onto. Bramble's hat brim
works the same way.

When a round says FLO or BLO, insert your hook under only that loop. The unused
loop stays visible as a ridge on the other side -- that ridge is the join line, and
it is meant to be there.

3 - The ruffle shell

Boo's hem repeats this group: 2 sl st, then (sc, hdc, dc, hdc, sc) all into the
next single stitch, then 1 sl st.

Five stitches into one stitch is what makes the fabric flare. Each repeat takes
4 stitches of the round and returns 8, so the edge doubles in length and ruffles.

Six repeats use all 24 stitches of Rnd 12 and produce 48 stitches -- six ruffles.

4 - Needle sculpting

This is what turns Pip from an orange ball into a pumpkin, and it is the reason
for the long tail you are told to leave.

You will run that tail through the stuffed body with a tapestry needle, entering
and exiting at the top and bottom, then pull and anchor it. Each pass pins a
channel into the surface. Six evenly spaced passes make six ribs.

The full method is in Pip's assembly, step by step. The short version: overstuff
first, because sculpting compresses the filling and a soft pumpkin will not hold
a rib.

1 - BOO THE RUFFLED GHOST
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked top-down in cream. Boo is an open bell -- he has no closed bottom and he
takes no stuffing. He stands on the weight of his ruffled hem.

R6   sc in each st around   (24)

front of the face. Fit the washers from inside.

With black floss, embroider a small curved smile centerd below the eyes. With pink
floss, add a short horizontal stitch either side for blush.

Pip - sculpting the ribs

Overstuff Pip before you start. Sculpting compresses the filling, and a soft
pumpkin will not hold a rib.

  1. Thread the 65 cm tail onto a tapestry needle.
  2. Find the center of the top (the R13 opening) and the center of the bottom (the
   R1 magic ring). These are your two anchor points. Every pass goes in one and
   out the other.
  3. Bring the needle down through the top center and out at the bottom center.
   Pull gently until the surface just begins to dimple -- do not strangle it.
  4. Move 6 stitches sideways along the bottom, bring the needle back up to the
   top center, and pull again. Anchor with a small stitch so the tension holds.
  5. Repeat, moving 6 stitches each time, until you have made 6 passes all the
   way around. Six passes on a 36-stitch body divides it into six even ribs.
  6. When the last rib is set, knot the tail against the surface, run the needle
   into the body and out the side, then pull the knot inside and trim.

Pull every rib to the same tension. One tight rib and five loose ones is the
most common way this goes wrong.

PATTERN 3 - DESIGN CODE NS 03 - AXEL THE AXOLOTL
================================================================
US crochet terms - Advanced beginner - 2.5-3 hours

A soft pink axolotl with six fluffy gills, a round head and a shell-edged paddle
tail. Head, neck, body and tail are worked as one continuous spiral, so there is
no neck seam to sew.

Axel measures about 11.5 cm (4.5 in) tall seated, about 10 cm (4 in) wide
gill tip to gill tip, with a 4.3 cm (1.7 in) tail. The head is a true sphere
at 52 mm wide x 51.6 mm tall.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SAFETY -- READ THIS FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------------

Axel has two 6 mm safety eyes. Safety eyes are small parts. Lock the washers
firmly from the inside before stuffing, and pull-test every eye on the finished
toy -- a properly locked washer will not come off by hand.

The gills, arms, feet and tail fin are all sewn on. Sew each seam twice and
weave every end in for at least 5 cm, then trim close. A loose gill is the first
thing a child will pull.

This pattern has not been tested to a toy-safety standard such as ASTM F963
or EN 71, so please do not describe finished Axels as "baby-safe" or "suitable
from birth" in your listings. For children under 3, embroidered eyes are the
safer choice: skip the safety eyes and stitch the eyes with black floss instead.


----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Main yarn -- Worsted #4, pale pink, about 15 g. Cotton or acrylic, smooth
matte finish. One 25 g ball covers the whole axolotl with plenty to spare.

Gill yarn -- Fuzzy / eyelash / fur yarn, dark pink, about 8 g. Essential:
worsted will not give fluffy gills, and the gills are the whole look.

Fin yarn -- Worsted #4, dark pink, about 3 g. Smooth yarn, so the shell
edging stays crisp.

Hook -- 3.5 mm (US E-4). Tight gauge so stuffing cannot show through.

Eyes -- 2 x 6 mm safety eyes. 9 mm reads as buggy on this head.

Also needed -- Polyfill about 8 g; tapestry needle; black embroidery
floss; pink pastel or chalk; stitch marker.


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE & FINISHED SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Gauge -- 36 sc around measures about 52 mm in diameter when stuffed
(4.5 mm per stitch, 4.3 mm per round).

Check it before you start: crochet 36 sc in the round for 3 rounds, stuff it,
and measure across. If it comes out wider than 55 mm, drop to a 3.0 mm hook; if
it comes out under 48 mm, go up to a 4.0 mm.

Finished size -- About 11.5 cm (4.5 in) tall seated, about 10 cm (4 in) wide
gill tip to gill tip, with a 4.3 cm (1.7 in) tail.


----------------------------------------------------------------

ABBREVIATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

MR magic ring - ch chain - sc single crochet - dc double crochet -
inc increase, 2 sc in one stitch - invdec invisible decrease -
sl st slip stitch - st(s) stitch(es) - FO fasten off -
(n) stitch count at the end of the round


----------------------------------------------------------------

CORE TECHNIQUES
----------------------------------------------------------------

These are the five techniques Axel uses, in the order you will meet them.

1 - THE MAGIC RING. Every piece begins with a magic ring. Wrap the yarn once
around two fingers, insert the hook, pull up a loop and work your first round
into the ring, then pull the tail to close it. Pull the tail tight once the
first round is complete.

2 - WORK IN A SPIRAL. Do not join and do not ch 1 between rounds -- just keep
going round after round. Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round
and move it up each time; there is no join to count from.

3 - THE INVISIBLE DECREASE. Insert the hook through the front loops only
of the next two stitches, yarn over and pull through both, then yarn over and
pull through the remaining two loops. This leaves no ridge, which matters on the
head and neck where the decreases are visible.

4 - CLOSING THROUGH BOTH LAYERS. To close a flat opening, fold the piece
flat so the front and back layers lie together, then work one sc through both
layers at a time. Axel's 6-stitch tail tip closes as 3 clean pairs.

5 - THE SHELL (SCALLOP). Work all the stitches of the group into one
stitch, then slip stitch into the next stitch to anchor it. The group fans out
into a cup. Working the dc loosely makes each scallop cup outward instead of
lying flat.


----------------------------------------------------------------

tip. Head and body are both 36 stitches around; the neck at R13 is the only

egg-shaped.

4. TAIL FIN -- SHELL EDGING

as a frown.

eye, just outside the eye line.

Gills. Sew 3 lobes to each side of the head, behind the eyes, anchored at
about R6 (upper), R7-R8 (middle) and R9 (lower). Angle the top lobe upward, the
middle one straight out and the bottom one downward so they fan. Then tease the
fibers apart with a slicker brush or your fingers.

center front, angled slightly forward.

Feet. Sew one foot to each side of the center front at R24-R25, about 4
stitches out from center, so Axel sits flat and upright.

TROUBLESHOOTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Head firm, body soft. Stuff the head hard at R11 so it holds a sphere and
the eyes stay level. Keep the body light so Axel stays squishy and sits down.

Neck collapsing. The 18-stitch neck is deliberately narrow. First try
packing a little more stuffing into the neck through the body opening before you
close R25. For a permanently sturdier neck, work R13 as [7 sc, inc] x2, 2 sc
(20) -- and then change R14 to [4 sc, inc] x4 (24) so the round consumes all 20
stitches. R15 onward is unchanged.

Fin runs out of tail. The ridge holds 10 stitches and 5 scallops need
exactly 10. If you shortened the tail, work fewer scallops rather than crowding
them.

Want a wider gill span? The gills as written give about 10 cm tip to tip.
Work each gill two rounds longer -- add 2 plain rounds at 12 stitches before R5 --
for a span of about 12 cm.

Stuffing shows through. Go down to a 3.0 mm hook. Loose gauge on a 3.5 mm
hook is the usual cause.

Axel will not sit. Move the feet one round lower and make sure the R23-R26
decreases are centered on the underside, not the back.

PATTERN 4 - DESIGN CODE NS 04 - COCO THE CAPYBARA
================================================================

A low, round, bottom-heavy capybara with a blunt sewn-on muzzle, plump stuffed

----------------------------------------------------------------

Work in continuous spiral rounds. Do not join at the end of a round and do
not chain 1 between rounds. Move a stitch marker into the first stitch of every
round -- in a spiral there is no join to count from, and the marker is the only
way to know where a round ends.

Unless a note says otherwise, work every stitch through both loops.


----------------------------------------------------------------

HOW IT COMES TOGETHER
----------------------------------------------------------------

The three techniques used throughout this pattern, in the order you meet them.

1 - The magic ring

Every piece begins with a magic ring. Pull the tail tight once the first round is
complete.

2 - Working in a spiral

Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round. There is no seam and no
join to count from.

3 - The two-layer leg join

This is the only genuinely fiddly step in the pattern.

Hold a finished leg against the body, with the pinched-flat top of the leg lying
against the outside of the body. Insert your hook through both the leg and
the body stitch, and work one single crochet. The leg edge and the body stitch
are treated as a single stitch.

Those stitches still count as one stitch each, so every stitch count in the body
table stays correct.

Pinching the leg top flat first is what makes this easy. A round, un-pinched leg
top will not lie flat against the curved body, and the join will pucker.


----------------------------------------------------------------

on.

    Work 7 sc.

between the two back legs, so her four feet cover the deepest footprint this
shape allows -- about 65 mm across and 29 mm from front to back.

Work the numbers exactly as printed. If the front legs end up directly behind the
back legs instead of between them, Coco's footprint narrows to about 9 mm from
front to back and she will tip over forwards and backwards.

Hold the piece upside down and check that all four legs sit square before you
stuff the body.


----------------------------------------------------------------

PART 3 -- WHY THE LEGS JOIN SO LOW
----------------------------------------------------------------

The back legs are 8 rounds long, about 34 mm, and they join at Rnd 4 -- only
17 mm above the base of the body. The front legs are 9 rounds long, about 39 mm,
and they join at Rnd 5, 21 mm up.

Different lengths, same result: every foot hangs 17 mm below the body, so all
four land level and the underside of the body clears the table by about that
much. Coco stands on her feet, not on her belly.

This is the single most common failure on a round-bodied animal. Legs joined at
the widest part of the body are always too high: the belly rests on the table and
the feet dangle in the air.

If Coco does not stand, check the join height before anything else.


----------------------------------------------------------------

PART 4 -- FACE AND ASSEMBLY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Muzzle

Stuff the muzzle lightly and pin it to the front of the head over Rnd 12-14,
centered on the face. Sew all the way around with matching brown.

The muzzle should sit proud of the head, not flush with it.

Centering matters. The muzzle is about 17 mm across and the eyes go 27 mm apart,
which leaves only about 5 mm of clear face between the edge of the muzzle and
each eye. A muzzle pinned even slightly off-center will crowd one eye.

Eyes

Embroider the eyes -- do not use safety eyes.

With dark brown, work a shallow downward arc about 3 stitches wide on each side,
at Rnd 14-15, 6 stitches apart, with a tiny tick angled down at each
outer end.

Six stitches keeps both eyes on the front of the face. Any wider and they wrap
around the sides. Placing them at Rnd 14-15 keeps the whole eye above the muzzle,
which is pinned over Rnd 12-14.

This closed-eye curve is what makes Coco look asleep.

Nose and mouth

Embroider the nose and mouth after the muzzle is sewn on, so the stitches sit
on the finished curve rather than on a flat piece.

On the muzzle, work a small dark triangle at the top center, then a short vertical
line down from it, and a soft curved mouth to one side.

Ears

Pinch the base of each ear so it cups forward, then sew at Rnd 15, about
5 stitches apart, angled slightly outward.

Rnd 15 is still a full 36-stitch round, so 5 stitches puts the ears on top of the
head where a capybara's ears belong. Sewing them wider apart -- or onto Rnd 16,
which has already decreased to 30 stitches -- pushes them out to the sides of the
head, where they disappear from the front.

Legs

The legs were joined during Rnd 4 and Rnd 5. There is nothing to sew. Just check
that the pinched 3-stitch strip of each leg is caught fully in the round.

Final shaping

Roll the finished piece gently between your palms to settle the stuffing into a
round, bottom-heavy shape.


----------------------------------------------------------------

TROUBLESHOOTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Coco looks too tall -- Almost always too many plain rounds. Rnd 12-15 is the
straight section of the head, and the face is positioned on it. Adding "just one
more" round each time turns the shape into a tower and moves the face off the
round it was designed for.

Coco will not stand -- Check the join height, not the leg spacing. The legs
belong on Rnd 4 and Rnd 5. Joined any higher, they cannot clear the underside of
the body.

Coco tips forward or backward -- Two causes, in order of likelihood. First,
the front legs are lined up behind the back legs instead of between them: the
Rnd 5 spacing must be 7 sc, 10 sc, 7 sc. Second, Coco is deliberately narrow from
front to back, at about 29 mm, and no leg spacing changes that. If she still
tips, stuff the lower body more firmly and settle her weight back over all four
feet.

Coco rocks back onto her haunches, front feet in the air -- All four legs are
the same length, and they cannot be. The front legs join at Rnd 5, 21 mm up, and
the back legs at Rnd 4, 17 mm up, so equal-length legs leave the back feet about
9 mm lower. Work the front legs 9 rounds and the back legs 8.

Coco will not sit level -- Stuff the lower body firmly. A soft base lets Coco
tip forward onto her muzzle.

The head flops back -- The waist at Rnd 10 is deliberately shallow, at 32
stitches. Do not decrease it further. A narrow neck cannot hold the head upright
on this shape.

The muzzle looks flat -- Stuff it just enough to hold a dome.

Stuffing shows through -- Your gauge is too loose. Thirty-six stitches should
measure 52 mm around a stuffed body. Crochet more tightly, or go down a hook
size.

There is a small hole where a leg meets the body -- You joined a flat, wide leg
top instead of a pinched one. Flatten the whole 9-stitch top into a strip about 3
stitches wide before joining, so the stitches you are not joining bunch up inside
the join.

Classic warm brown, soft grey, sandy beige, and cocoa.

PATTERN 5 - DESIGN CODE NS 05 - LITTLE DUCK PLUSHIE
================================================================
  (design code restored from the source file name; the
   source file carried no design-code line)
A round, squashy duckling worked in super-bulky chenille as a single piece from
tail to crown, so there is no body-to-head seam. Only the wings and the beak
are sewn on.


----------------------------------------------------------------

AT A GLANCE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Finished size  about 16 cm (6.25 in) tall, 7.5 cm (3 in) wide
-------------  ----------------------------------------------
Yarn           super-bulky chenille (#6)
Hook           4.5 mm (US 7)
Skill          confident beginner
Time           1.5-2 hours
Pieces to sew  2 wings + 1 beak


----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

  - Body yarn: super-bulky chenille (#6), yellow -- about 25-35 g. A 100 g
  ball makes two ducks comfortably, and three if your tension is on the tight
  side.
  - Details: a small amount of orange yarn for the beak; black yarn or
  embroidery floss for the eyes.
  - Hook: 4.5 mm (US 7)
  - Fiberfill: about 25-35 g, packed firmly
  - Other: yarn needle, stitch marker, pins


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE AND FINISHED SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Gauge. In super-bulky chenille (#6) on a 4.5 mm hook: about 8 mm per
stitch and 7 mm per round.

Chenille varies a great deal between brands. Measure a 12-stitch swatch -- about
10 cm -- before you commit, because the finished height depends entirely on this.

Finished size. About 16 cm / 6.25 in tall and 7.5 cm / 3 in wide at
the gauge above. Those figures come straight from the round count: 23 rounds at
7 mm, and a widest point of 30 stitches at 8 mm.

Making a smaller duck

The same pattern in a lighter yarn, on a 3.0 mm hook:

Yarn                     Finished height
-----------------------  ---------------
Velvet / bulky (#5)      10-12 cm
Aran / worsted (#4)      9-10 cm
DK / light worsted (#3)  7.5-8.5 cm

DK is often suggested for this, but it is fine enough that the duck lands at
about 8 cm rather than 10-12 cm -- a perfectly good result, just not the size
most people expect. Choose velvet if the 10-12 cm size is what you want.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SKILL LEVEL
----------------------------------------------------------------

Confident beginner.

Techniques you will use

  1. Magic ring -- the start of the body and each wing.
  2. Working in a continuous spiral, with a stitch marker in the first stitch
   of every round. There is no join to count from.
  3. Invisible decreases -- see the note at the end of the pattern.
  4. Working around both sides of a starting chain -- the beak. You work along
   one side, round the end, and back along the other.
  5. Working through both layers at once to close the flat edge of each wing.
  6. Embroidery for the eyes.
  7. Sewing on the wings and the beak.

3. BEAK -- MAKE 1
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked around both sides of a starting chain of 5. Each side works into four
chains -- the three along the side plus the corner chain -- and between them all
five chains are used, so nothing is left unworked.

Ch 5.

Rnd 1. Starting in the 2nd ch from hook: sc in next 3 ch, 3 sc in the last
ch. Rotate to work along the underside of the chain: sc in next 3, 2 sc in the
last loop. [11]

Rnd 2. inc, 2 sc, inc, 2 sc, inc, 2 sc, inc, sc. [15]

Sl st in the next st and FO, leaving a long tail.

    How the chain is used. Side one takes 3 sc plus a 3-st corner into the
    final chain, touching four chains. Side two takes 3 sc plus a 2-st corner into
    the final loop, again touching four. The middle three chains are worked twice,
    once from each side. Rnd 1 therefore totals 11 stitches. Rnd 2's four increases
    and seven plain stitches consume all 11 and produce 15.

Do not stuff the beak. Flatten it before pinning so it sits as a flat lens
rather than a puffy tube.

About the size. As written the beak finishes about 50 mm long, which is
two-thirds of the head width -- a bold, cartoon beak that suits a duckling well.
For a daintier duck, work Rnd 1 only and finish there: 11 stitches gives a
beak about 37 mm long.

At this gauge that is 56-64 mm on a 76 mm head, which leaves comfortable room at
the edges.

around the outer edge.

----------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Stuff the body firmly from R10 as the waist begins to close -- it is much
   harder to reach once the neck narrows.
  2. Embroider the eyes before the head is fully closed, while you can still
   reach inside to bury the ends.
  3. Sew the beak across R16-R18, centered between the eyes.
  4. Sew the wings to R6-R9, one on each side, angled slightly back.
  5. Top up the stuffing through R22 and close.

INVISIBLE DECREASE
----------------------------------------------------------------

An invisible decrease gives a flatter join than a standard decrease, which shows
at this scale.

  1. Insert your hook under the front loop only of the next stitch.
  2. Insert your hook under the front loop only of the stitch after that.
   You now have three loops on the hook.
  3. Yarn over and pull through the first two loops.
  4. Yarn over and pull through both remaining loops.

PATTERN 6 - DESIGN CODE NS 06 - MOMO THE LOAF CAT
================================================================
US crochet terms - Advanced beginner - No-sew body - 2-2.5 hours

A cat folded into a perfect loaf. Worked in one piece on an oval base so the
finished shape is genuinely wider than it is tall, with the ears crocheted
straight onto the head and the tail worked off the body -- there are no separate
pieces to sew on.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SAFETY -- READ THIS FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------------

Momo has two 8 mm safety eyes. Safety eyes are small parts. Lock the washers
firmly from the inside and pull-test each one on the finished toy -- a properly
locked washer will not come off by hand.

Embroidering the eyes instead is safer, but it does not make Momo a tested
toy. This pattern has not been tested to a toy-safety standard such as
ASTM F963 or EN 71, so please do not describe finished Momos as "baby-safe" or
"suitable from birth" in your listings. For a child under three, embroidered
eyes are the right choice -- but say "embroidered, no small parts", not
"baby-safe".

The tail and ears are worked into the body fabric rather than sewn on, which is
genuinely stronger than a sewn join. Still weave every end in for at least 5 cm
and bury it, and knot the embroidery floss inside the body.


----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Main yarn -- Worsted #4, about 10 g. Grey, ginger, cream or black -- all
four sell equally well. One 25 g ball makes two with yarn to spare.

Contrast -- Small amounts of white for the chest and paws, pink for the nose
and inner ears.

Hook -- 3.5 mm (US E-4). Tight gauge so stuffing cannot show through.

Eyes -- 2 x 8 mm safety eyes, or embroider in black.

Also needed -- Polyfill about 10 g; tapestry needle; stitch marker; black
embroidery floss.


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE & FINISHED SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Gauge -- Worsted #4 on a 3.5 mm hook: about 4.5 mm per stitch and 4.3 mm per
round.

Finished size -- About 7.9 cm (3.1 in) long, 5.2 cm (2 in) wide and 4.3 cm
(1.7 in) tall -- a low, wide loaf, not a ball. Width to height is about 1.2 : 1,
which is what reads as a loaf.


----------------------------------------------------------------

ABBREVIATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

MR magic ring - ch chain - sc single crochet - inc 2 sc in one
stitch - dec invisible decrease - sl st slip stitch - BLO back loop
only - FO fasten off - (n) stitch count

Work in a continuous spiral -- do not join between rounds. Mark the first stitch
of every round.


----------------------------------------------------------------

CORE TECHNIQUES
----------------------------------------------------------------

These are the techniques Momo uses, in the order you will meet them.

1 - WORKING AROUND A CHAIN (the oval base). This is the one that makes the
loaf. You crochet into both sides of a starting chain: work across the front
of the chain, put the corner increases into the last chain, then continue back
along the opposite side and finish into the last remaining loop. That turns a
chain into a flat oval instead of a strip. Do not substitute a magic-ring circle
here -- a round base gives you a sphere every time.

2 - THE MAGIC RING. Used for the tail's first round. Wrap the yarn once
around two fingers, insert the hook, pull up a loop, work the round into the
ring, then pull the tail tight.

3 - WORK IN A SPIRAL. Do not join and do not ch 1 between rounds. Keep a
stitch marker in the first stitch of every round and move it up each time --
there is no join to count from.

4 - BACK LOOP ONLY (BLO). Work into the far loop of the stitch only. The
unused loops form a raised ridge -- that ridge is the visible edge where the base
meets the walls, and it is what makes the loaf read as folded rather than
moulded.

5 - THE INVISIBLE DECREASE. Insert the hook through the front loops only
of the next two stitches, yarn over and pull through both, then yarn over and
pull through the remaining two loops. Every dec here is worked this way.

6 - FLAT ROWS (ch 1, turn). The ears are not worked in the round. Chain 1,
turn, and work back along the row you just made. Each row is shorter than the
one before, which is what forms the ear point.

7 - WORKING INTO BODY FABRIC. The tail starts by pushing the hook through
the finished body wall rather than into a stitch of the current round. Go under
a whole stitch, not just one loop, or it will pull out.


----------------------------------------------------------------

1. BODY -- ONE PIECE, WORKED ON AN OVAL BASE
----------------------------------------------------------------

The oval base is what makes this a loaf rather than a ball, and the size of
the base sets the size of the whole cat. The base is worked up to 48 stitches
before the walls begin; stopping at 36 gives a body about 34 mm across, which
comes out taller than it is wide and reads as a ball no matter how you stuff it.

Foundation  ch 9                                                                                    chain only
Rnd 1       sc in 2nd ch from hook, sc in next 6, 3 sc in last ch, sc in next 6, 2 sc in last loop  (18)        worked around both sides of the chain
Rnd 2       inc, 6 sc, inc x3, 6 sc, inc x2                                                         (24)
Rnd 3       sc, inc, 6 sc, [sc, inc] x3, 6 sc, [sc, inc] x2                                         (30)
Rnd 4       2 sc, inc, 6 sc, [2 sc, inc] x3, 6 sc, [2 sc, inc] x2                                   (36)
Rnd 5       3 sc, inc, 6 sc, [3 sc, inc] x3, 6 sc, [3 sc, inc] x2                                   (42)
Rnd 6       4 sc, inc, 6 sc, [4 sc, inc] x3, 6 sc, [4 sc, inc] x2                                   (48)        base at full size
Rnd 7       BLO sc around                                                                           (48)        ridge = the base edge
Rnd 8       sc in each st around                                                                    (48)        eyes at Rnd 8-9
Rnd 9       sc in each st around                                                                    (48)        stuff firmly from here
Rnd 10      [6 sc, dec] x6                                                                          (42)
Rnd 11      [5 sc, dec] x6                                                                          (36)
Rnd 12      [4 sc, dec] x6                                                                          (30)        ears worked onto Rnd 12
Rnd 13      [3 sc, dec] x6                                                                          (24)
Rnd 14      [2 sc, dec] x6                                                                          (18)
Rnd 15      [sc, dec] x6                                                                            (12)        top up the stuffing
Rnd 16      dec x6                                                                                  (6)

Finish. Thread the tail through the front loop of each remaining stitch,
pull tight, knot and bury the end.

Shape check. The base is an oval about 79 mm long and 52 mm across; the
walls add ten rounds of height, so the finished loaf is about 43 mm tall --
wider than it is tall, which is the whole point. If yours is coming out round,
either you stopped increasing at Rnd 4 instead of Rnd 6, or you worked extra
plain rounds at Rnds 8-9.


----------------------------------------------------------------

2. EARS -- CROCHETED DIRECTLY ONTO THE HEAD, MAKE 2
----------------------------------------------------------------

No separate pieces. Join the main yarn to the head at Rnd 12, leaving about
6 stitches between the two ears, and work each ear as three short rows.

RND    INSTRUCTION   STS  NOTE
-----  ------------  ---  ----------
Row 1  sc in next 5  (5)  ch 1, turn
Row 2  dec, sc, dec  (3)  ch 1, turn
Row 3  dec, sc       (2)  ear tip

Finish. FO, leaving a short tail. Pull the tail through the 2 tip stitches
to close the point, then bury it inside the head.

Each row consumes exactly what the row before produced: 5 stitches into Row 2
(2 + 1 + 2), then 3 stitches into Row 3 (2 + 1).

Embroider a small pink triangle on the front of each ear if you want lined ears.

----------------------------------------------------------------

Join the main yarn to the back of the body at about Rnd 9, on the center
line.

RND    INSTRUCTION           STS  NOTE
-----  --------------------  ---  ---------------------------
Rnd 1  4 sc in MR            (4)  worked into the body fabric
Rnd 2  sc in each st around  (4)
Rnd 3  sc in each st around  (4)
Rnd 4  sc in each st around  (4)
Rnd 5  sc in each st around  (4)
Rnd 6  sc in each st around  (4)
Rnd 7  sc in each st around  (4)
Rnd 8  sc in each st around  (4)

Finish. FO. Do not stuff -- a thin tail curls around the loaf far better than
a stuffed one.

To start the tail on the body: insert the hook under a stitch, pull up a loop,
and work the first 4 sc into the body fabric in a tight square. From Rnd 2 you
are working in a normal spiral off those 4 stitches.


----------------------------------------------------------------

4. FACE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Eyes. Fix the 8 mm safety eyes between Rnds 8 and 9, about 7 stitches apart,
low and wide on the front of the loaf. Seven stitches is about 32 mm, roughly
60% of the body width. A loaf cat's eyes sit low -- high eyes make it read as a
bear.

Nose. Embroider a small pink triangle centered between and just below the
eyes, then two short stitches angled down from the point to make the mouth.

Whisker dots. Three tiny black French knots on each side of the nose.

Chest and paws. With white yarn, embroider a soft oval on the chest and two
small ovals at the front of the base for tucked paws. This is what turns a plain
loaf into a cat.


----------------------------------------------------------------

5. COLORWAYS THAT SELL
----------------------------------------------------------------

Grey tabby, orange ginger, cream, black, and calico (grey base with ginger and
cream patches embroidered on afterwards). Listing all five as photographs in one
listing consistently outperforms listing them separately, because buyers are
choosing a cat, not a pattern.

TROUBLESHOOTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

It came out round. You stopped increasing at Rnd 4 instead of Rnd 6, worked
too many plain rounds at Rnds 8-9, or used a magic-ring base instead of the
oval. The base must reach 48 stitches.

It came out tall and narrow. The base did not reach 48 stitches. Count the
stitches at Rnd 6 before you start the BLO round -- that one number decides the
whole shape.

Ears lean backwards. Join the yarn one round lower and angle the first row
slightly forward.

Tail falls off. The first 4 sc must go through the body fabric, not just
under one loop.

Stuffing shows. Go down to a 3.0 mm hook. Loose gauge on 3.5 mm is the usual
cause.

Face looks like a bear. The eyes are too high and too close. Drop them a
round and widen to 7 stitches.

THE FINISHED PROJECT
----------------------------------------------------------------

Momo sitting in a proper loaf -- long, low and wide.

Before you finish. This is a no-sew pattern, so there is nothing to lay out
and assemble. Instead, check three things before you close the top: the base
reached 48 stitches at Rnd 6, the BLO ridge runs all the way around in one
unbroken line, and the ears are level with each other and about 6 stitches
apart.

Sunny the Sunflower - Waddle the Penguin - Spud the Potato

Three palm-sized amigurumi designed to slip into a coat pocket. They work up
fast -- an evening or two for all three -- and make excellent small gift add-ons,
party favours, or a gentle first project.

The trio shares one body: a six-stitch sphere for Waddle, a six-stitch
cushion for Sunny, and a flat oval for Spud. Once you have worked the
first toy, the other two will feel familiar.


----------------------------------------------------------------

Finished size  about 3.0 cm (1.2 in) across the petals  about 3.8 cm (1.5 in) tall  about 2.7 cm (1.1 in) long
Yarn weight    #4 worsted                               #4 worsted                  #4 worsted
Hook           3.5 mm (US E/4)                          3.5 mm (US E/4)             3.5 mm (US E/4)
Time per toy   20-30 minutes                            20-30 minutes               25-35 minutes

Sunny and Waddle have no separate pieces to sew. Spud is worked flat, then the

----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

  - Yarn: worsted weight / #4 (about 250 m per 100 g). The three toys use
  roughly 8 m of yarn in total -- under 4 g -- so almost any scrap will do.
    - Sunny -- golden yellow for the petals, chocolate brown for the center
    - Waddle -- black, plus cream for the chest patch and yellow for the beak
    - Spud -- warm tan
  - Hook: 3.5 mm (US E/4)
  - Eyes: 5 mm black safety eyes x6 (or embroidery thread -- see *Safety and
  selling*, below)
  - Fiberfill: about 3 g in total for all three, or enough to fill each body
  firmly without lumps
  - Blunt tapestry needle, stitch marker, scissors


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Approximately 3.5 mm per stitch and 3.2 mm per round in single crochet
worked in the round with the stated yarn and hook. Amigurumi is normally worked
a half-size tighter than the yarn calls for.

This is a pocket-size set, so gauge matters: a single half-size difference
changes the finished size noticeably. Work a gauge swatch before you start.
The sizes in the table above are calculated from this gauge.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SKILL LEVEL
----------------------------------------------------------------

Easy / beginner-friendly.

Techniques you will use

  1. Magic ring -- the starting loop for Sunny's center and Waddle's body.
  2. Working in a continuous spiral with a stitch marker, rather than joining
   each round.
  3. Invisible decreases (see the note at the end of the pattern).
  4. Clusters worked into one stitch -- Sunny's petals. Three stitches are
   worked into a single stitch of the round below, which makes them stand out
   from the center.
  5. Slip stitch edging between the petals, which flattens the joins.
  6. Oval base worked around a chain -- Spud's potato. You work along one side
   of a foundation chain, around the end, back along the other side, and round
   the other end, increasing at both ends.
  7. Sewing and embroidery for Waddle's wings, chest and beak, and Sunny's
   center and face.

MAKING A BIGGER TRIO
----------------------------------------------------------------

The three finished sizes above are worked in the round with #4 worsted on a
3.5 mm hook. If you want the toys noticeably larger, switch to a 3.5 mm hook
with chunky or aran weight yarn, or work #4 worsted held double:

  - at about 4.5 mm per stitch everything scales up by roughly 1.3x
  - Sunny about 3.9 cm across the petals
  - Waddle about 4.9 cm tall
  - Spud about 3.5 cm long

No stitch counts change -- the same rounds give the same shapes at the new size.
Make a gauge swatch first, because yarns vary.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SAFETY AND SELLING
----------------------------------------------------------------

This set is not intended for children under 3. Safety eyes are small parts
that can be pulled off and swallowed. Even with safety washers fitted, a
determined child can work an eye loose.

If you are making these for young children, embroider the eyes and mouths with
yarn instead of using safety eyes. Embroidered eyes look just as good at this
scale and remove the hazard entirely.

A note for sellers:

  - Do not market these as "baby-safe." The term has no legal definition in
  toy safety, and using it as a listing filter invites a claim you cannot
  support. Toys sold to children in the EU must carry CE marking under EN 71,
  and in the United States must meet the CPSIA lead and phthalate limits and
  ASTM F963. Independent testing is the only way to know whether your toys
  meet those standards.
  - Sell them instead as adult collectibles, desk companions, or keychain
  charms -- the pocket-size format suits that framing well.
  - Include a hang tag or listing line with your materials, your name as maker,
  and "Not suitable for children under 3 years."
  - Stuff firmly and evenly. Lumpy or under-stuffed toys read as cheap and lose
  their shape within weeks.

----------------------------------------------------------------

INVISIBLE DECREASE
----------------------------------------------------------------

An invisible decrease (inv dec) produces a flatter join than a standard
decrease, which matters at this scale.

  1. Insert your hook under the front loop only of the next stitch.
  2. Insert your hook under the front loop only of the stitch after that.
   You should have three loops on the hook.
  3. Yarn over and pull through the first two loops.
  4. Yarn over and pull through both remaining loops.


----------------------------------------------------------------

CARE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Spot clean with a damp cloth and mild soap; do not machine wash. Keep away from
heat. Surface-fluff any pulled loops with a clean pet slicker brush or a toothbrush.

A chunky, big-headed baby dragon with a stubby snout, scalloped wings and a ridge

WORKING NOTES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Work in a continuous spiral unless a row says to turn. Mark the first stitch of
every round -- in a spiral there is no join to count from.

The wings and the spike strip are the exceptions: both are worked flat, in turned
rows.

Unless a note says otherwise, work every stitch through both loops.


----------------------------------------------------------------

HOW IT COMES TOGETHER
----------------------------------------------------------------

The core techniques, in the order you meet them.

1 - The magic ring. Every piece begins with a magic ring. Pull the tail tight
once the first round is complete.

2 - Work in a spiral. Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round.

3 - Matched open joins. Ember's head and her neck are both left open at
18 stitches, so the two edges are the same size and can be ladder-stitched
together cleanly. This is the single most important structural detail in the
pattern -- the head is heavy, and this joint carries all of it.


----------------------------------------------------------------

1 - HEAD -- MAIN COLOR, MAKE 1
----------------------------------------------------------------

A big head is what makes a dragon read as a baby dragon. Two straight rounds
only, then the decreases.

The head is worked top-down and is left open. Do not close it.

RND  INSTRUCTION            STS   NOTE
---  ---------------------  ----  ------------------------------
R1   6 sc in MR             (6)
R2   inc in each st around  (12)
R3   [sc, inc] x6           (18)
R4   [2 sc, inc] x6         (24)
R5   [3 sc, inc] x6         (30)
R6   [4 sc, inc] x6         (36)  head at full width
R7   sc in each st around   (36)  eyes go on R7-R8
R8   sc in each st around   (36)
R9   [4 sc, dec] x6         (30)
R10  [3 sc, dec] x6         (24)  stuff firmly
R11  [2 sc, dec] x6         (18)  leave open -- matches the neck

Finish. Stuff the head firmly. Do not fasten off and do not close it. The
open 18-stitch edge is sewn to the body's neck. Leave a long tail if you prefer to
sew rather than join as you go.

    This is where the original shape of the pattern was weakest. Closing the head
    down to 6 stitches leaves a gathered point about 9 mm across, and sewing that to
    a 26 mm neck ring is a small seam carrying a heavy head -- which is exactly why a
    dragon's head flops. Stopping at 18 stitches gives the two pieces edges that
    match, and the join becomes strong.

-----  ------  ------  --------  ------------
back   6       26 mm   26 mm up  the table
front  8       34 mm   34 mm up  the table

Make all four the same length and the back feet end up 9 mm lower than the front
ones, so Ember rocks back onto her haunches with her front feet in the air.

Sew the back legs to Rnd 6 and the front legs to Rnd 8, about 8 stitches
apart on each pair. Angle the back legs under her as haunches and the front legs
slightly forward. Ember's body rests on the table; the legs support the pose rather
than lifting her off it.


----------------------------------------------------------------

5 - TAIL -- MAIN COLOR, MAKE 1
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked from the tip up, so it tapers naturally.

RND  INSTRUCTION           STS
---  --------------------  ----
R1   4 sc in MR            (4)
R2   sc in each st around  (4)
R3   [sc, inc] x2          (6)
R4   sc in each st around  (6)
R5   sc in each st around  (6)
R6   [2 sc, inc] x2        (8)
R7   sc in each st around  (8)
R8   sc in each st around  (8)
R9   [3 sc, inc] x2        (10)
R10  sc in each st around  (10)
R11  sc in each st around  (10)
R12  sc in each st around  (10)

Finish. Stuff lightly. FO with a long tail. Flatten the open end and sew it to
the back of the body at Rnds 4-6.


----------------------------------------------------------------

6 - HORNS -- CONTRAST COLOR, MAKE 2
----------------------------------------------------------------

RND  INSTRUCTION           STS
---  --------------------  ---
R1   4 sc in MR            (4)
R2   sc in each st around  (4)
R3   [sc, inc] x2          (6)
R4   sc in each st around  (6)
R5   sc in each st around  (6)

Finish. Do not stuff. FO with a long tail. Sew to the crown of the head,
6 stitches apart, angled back.


----------------------------------------------------------------

7 - WINGS -- CONTRAST COLOR, MAKE 2
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked flat, in turned rows, then finished with a scalloped edge. Every row
consumes exactly the stitches the row before it produced.

Ch 11.

Row 1. From the 2nd ch from the hook: sc in 4, hdc in 3, dc in 3. Ch 1, turn.
[10]

Row 2. sc in 3, hdc in 3, dc in 4. Ch 1, turn. [10]

Row 3. sc in 2, hdc in 4, dc in 4. Ch 1, turn. [10]

Row 4 -- scalloped edge. sl st in the first 2, (sc, hdc, dc, hdc, sc) in the
next st, sl st in the next 2, (sc, hdc, dc, hdc, sc) in the next st, sl st in
the last 4.

Finish. FO with a 25 cm tail.

The slip-stitch groups are 2 / 2 / 4 -- eight stitches -- which with the two
shells, worked into one stitch each, consumes all 10 stitches of Row 3. Nothing is
left over. Both scallops are identical, so the two ends of the wing match.

Pin the straight inner edge of each wing to the back of the body across
Rnds 10-11, about 8 stitches apart, angled up and out. Sew along the whole
straight edge so the scalloped edge stays free -- sewing only the top corner is what
makes a wing droop.


----------------------------------------------------------------

8 - BACK SPIKES -- CONTRAST COLOR, MAKE 1
----------------------------------------------------------------

One continuous strip, sewn down the center back from the crown of the head to the
tip of the tail. Nine spikes: 3 on the head, 4 on the body, 2 on the tail.

Ch 4.

Row 1. sc in the 2nd ch from the hook. Then work this group 9 times:

    ch 4, sl st in the 2nd ch from the hook, sc in the next ch, hdc in the next ch.

Each group makes one spike, and the last stitch of each group is also where the
next one begins -- so the nine spikes form one continuous ridge with no gaps.

Then sc in the next 2, and FO with a long tail.

The finished strip is 30 stitches -- 1 at the start, 3 for each of the 9 spikes,
2 at the end -- and roughly 13.5 cm long. That is a little under the 15.5 cm crown-to-tail
path, which is what you want: the strip follows a curve over the head, down the
back and along the tail, and it should go on slightly snug rather than stretched.
If it will not reach, work one more spike.

    Each spike is a small cone. Chaining 4 and working back along it with a slip
    stitch, a single and a half double makes a point about 10 mm long, and about
    13 mm of the strip is taken up by each one. Nine of them plus the ends come to
    roughly 148 mm.
    
    The spikes deliberately touch. A dragon's spine is a continuous ridge, and any
    plain stitches you add between them make the strip too long for the path -- each
    extra single crochet adds about 4.5 mm.

Check the length before you sew. Pin the strip from the crown down the back to
the tail tip. If it runs long, unpick from the plain end; if it runs short, work
one more spike, which adds about 13.5 mm. Sewing it slightly stretched or slightly gathered is visible from
across the room, so get this right before the first stitch.


----------------------------------------------------------------

9 - ASSEMBLY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Work in this order. Each step is easier before the next one is in the way.

  1. Snout to head, centered on Rnds 8-11, below the eye line.
  2. Eyes between Rnds 7 and 8, 8 stitches apart, just above the snout. Fit
   the washers before the head is joined to the body -- once it is closed up you
   cannot reach inside.
  3. Horns to the crown, 6 stitches apart, angled back.
  4. Head to body. Both edges are open at 18 stitches. Seat the head on the neck
   and ladder-stitch all the way around, then make a second pass and pull it
   tight. Pack the neck firmly first. This joint carries the whole head.
  5. Legs -- back pair to Rnd 6, front pair to Rnd 8, angled as described.
  6. Tail to the back of the body at Rnds 4-6.
  7. Wings across Rnds 10-11.
  8. Spike strip last, pinned from crown to tail tip before you sew a stitch.


----------------------------------------------------------------

TROUBLESHOOTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Head flops forward -- Two causes, and both are about the join. Pack the neck
firmly before you close it, and make the second ladder-stitch pass tight. If the
head still moves, the two edges did not match: both the head and the neck must be
open at 18 stitches.

Ember rocks back onto her haunches -- All four legs are the same length, and
they cannot be. The back legs attach 26 mm up and the front legs 34 mm up, so
equal-length legs leave the back feet 9 mm lower. Work the back legs 6 rounds and
the front legs 8.

Ember will not sit -- The legs are sewn too high, or the body is under-stuffed
at the base. Back legs belong on Rnd 6 and front legs on Rnd 8, and the lower body
needs to be firm enough to sit on.

Wings droop -- Sew along the whole straight inner edge, not just the top corner.

Spike strip curves -- Pin the entire strip from crown to tail before you sew a
single stitch. This is the most visible seam on the toy.

Spike strip is the wrong length -- The strip is worked from a 40-chain
foundation, which suits this gauge. If your chains are looser it will run long;
unpick from the plain end rather than stretching it to fit.

Looks like a bear -- The snout is missing or under-stuffed. It must project past
the dome of the head.

Stuffing shows through -- Your gauge is too loose. Thirty stitches should
measure 43 mm around the body. Crochet more tightly, or go down to a 3.0 mm hook.


----------------------------------------------------------------

COLORWAYS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Sage green, dusty teal, lilac, charcoal and blush pink all sell well. Jewel tones
photograph best against a pale background.


----------------------------------------------------------------

BEFORE YOU ASSEMBLE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Lay every component out and check it against the pattern before you sew anything:

  - 1 head, open at 18 stitches
  - 1 snout
  - 1 body, neck open at 18 stitches
  - 2 back legs of 6 rounds, 2 front legs of 8 rounds
  - 1 tail
  - 2 horns
  - 2 wings
  - 1 spike strip with 9 spikes

Pin each piece and look at Ember from the front before committing a single stitch.

PATTERN 9 - DESIGN CODE NS 09 - SHELBY THE SEA TURTLE BAG CHARM
================================================================
US crochet terms - Easy - 30 minutes - market bestseller

A pocket sea turtle on a keyring -- the classic craft-market impulse buy. Under
half an hour, a couple of grams of yarn, and the flippers and head are worked
straight into the shell so there is almost nothing to sew.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SAFETY -- READ THIS FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------------

Shelby has two 4 mm safety eyes. Safety eyes are small parts. Lock the
washers firmly from the inside and pull-test each one -- a properly locked washer
will not come off by hand.

This pattern has not been tested to a toy-safety standard such as ASTM F963
or EN 71. Shelby is sold as a keyring and bag charm; if you also market it as a
pocket toy, please do not describe it as "baby-safe" or "suitable from
birth". For a child under three, use French knots rather than safety eyes.

The keyring is the other hazard: attach it through two stitches and knot the
yarn inside the shell. A charm that comes off a bag is a small part with a metal
ring on it.


----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Shell yarn -- DK / light worsted (#3) cotton, about 1 g. Sage, teal,
mustard or rust.

Body yarn -- DK cotton, about 1 g in a contrast cream, sand or pale green.

The whole charm is 203 stitches, which is roughly 3 m of DK. Two grams covers
it with room for the embroidery and the tails; the rest of a ball makes the next
ten charms.

Hook -- 2.5 mm (US C-2).

Eyes -- 2 x 4 mm safety eyes, or two small black French knots. See the note in
section 4 -- at this size the knots are the easier fit.

Also needed -- A pinch of polyfill (well under 1 g); tapestry needle; one
25 mm keyring or lobster clasp per turtle.


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE & FINISHED SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Gauge -- DK cotton on a 2.5 mm hook: about 3.5 mm per stitch and 3.2 mm per
round.

Finished size -- About 3.7 cm (1.5 in) across the flippers, 3.2 cm (1.25 in)
long and 1.9 cm (0.75 in) deep. Both the shell and the underside finish at 24
stitches, and 24 stitches at this gauge is a disc 26.7 mm across -- that single
number sets the size of the whole charm. If you want a 6 cm charm, see Making
a bigger Shelby at the end.


----------------------------------------------------------------

ABBREVIATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

MR magic ring - sc single crochet - hdc half double crochet -
inc 2 sc in one stitch - sl st slip stitch - BLO back loop only -
FO fasten off - (n) stitch count

Work in a continuous spiral -- do not join between rounds. Mark the first stitch
of every round.


----------------------------------------------------------------

CORE TECHNIQUES
----------------------------------------------------------------

These are the techniques Shelby uses, in the order you will meet them.

1 - THE MAGIC RING. Wrap the yarn once around two fingers, insert the hook,
pull up a loop, work the first round into the ring, then pull the tail tight.

2 - WORK IN A SPIRAL. Do not join and do not ch 1 between rounds. Keep a
stitch marker in the first stitch of every round and move it up each time --
there is no join to count from.

3 - BACK LOOP ONLY (BLO). Work into the far loop of the stitch only. The
unused loops form a raised ridge -- on the shell that ridge is the rim, and it is
what makes the dome read as a shell rather than a bun.

4 - SEVERAL STITCHES INTO ONE STITCH (the cluster). This is the whole trick
of the head and flippers. You work every stitch of the group into one stitch
of the previous round, so they all share a single anchor and fan out into a bump
that stands proud of the fabric. Each cluster consumes one stitch and produces
as many stitches as it contains -- that is why a round of 24 stitches can produce
35.

5 - WHIP STITCH. Pass the needle through both layers from back to front,
then over the edge and back through, repeating. Keep the tension even or the
join will pucker.

6 - FRENCH KNOTS. Bring the needle up, wrap the floss once or twice around
the needle, then push the needle back down close to where it emerged and pull
through slowly. Used for the eyes at this size.


----------------------------------------------------------------

1. SHELL -- TOP
----------------------------------------------------------------

A shallow dome. The BLO round at Rnd 5 gives the shell its rim.

RND    INSTRUCTION            STS   NOTE
-----  ---------------------  ----  ---------
Rnd 1  6 sc in MR             (6)
Rnd 2  inc in each st around  (12)
Rnd 3  [sc, inc] x6           (18)
Rnd 4  [2 sc, inc] x6         (24)
Rnd 5  BLO sc around          (24)  rim ridge
Rnd 6  sc in each st around   (24)

Finish. FO, leaving a 30 cm tail for joining.

Shell markings. Before joining, embroider five or six shallow V shapes in a
darker shade across the dome. It takes two minutes and it is the difference
between a turtle and a green blob.


----------------------------------------------------------------

2. UNDERSIDE -- WITH HEAD AND FLIPPERS WORKED IN
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked as a flat disc in the contrast color. The head and four flippers are
worked directly into the final round, so there is nothing to sew on afterwards.

RND    INSTRUCTION            STS   NOTE
-----  ---------------------  ----  ----------------------------
Rnd 1  6 sc in MR             (6)
Rnd 2  inc in each st around  (12)
Rnd 3  [sc, inc] x6           (18)
Rnd 4  [2 sc, inc] x6         (24)  flat disc, matches the shell

Finish. Do not fasten off -- work the head and flippers next.


----------------------------------------------------------------

3. HEAD & FLIPPERS -- WORKED INTO RND 4 OF THE UNDERSIDE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Five bumps worked into the 24 stitches of the underside. Each bump takes one
stitch, and the plain stitches between them space the limbs evenly.

RND    INSTRUCTION                                                                                                                                                                STS   NOTE
-----  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------  ----  -----------------
Rnd 5  3 sc, (sc, hdc, hdc, sc) in next st, 4 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st, 3 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st, 4 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st, 3 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st, 2 sc  (35)  head + 4 flippers

Finish. Sl st in the next stitch and FO, leaving a 20 cm tail.

The round consumes 3 + 1 + 4 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 4 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 24 stitches,
exactly closing the underside disc, and produces 35. The first bump -- the
four-stitch one -- is the head; the four smaller bumps are the flippers.

The five bumps land on stitches 4, 9, 13, 18 and 22, which puts the two front
flippers 75° and 90° either side of the head. That one-stitch difference is
invisible at this size, but if you want them exact, work the last plain run as
3 sc instead of 2 and take one stitch from the run before the head.


----------------------------------------------------------------

4. FACE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Eyes -- read this first. The head bump is four stitches worked into a single
stitch, so it is a small loop only about 7-10 mm across, and a 4 mm safety eye
needs a washer of roughly 6-7 mm. The washer often will not fit inside the bump,
and two eyes 2 stitches apart (7 mm) sit right at its edges.

At this size, French knots are the better choice. Two small black knots,
2 stitches apart, low on the front of the head bump.

If you do want safety eyes, place them 1 stitch apart and check the washer
seats fully before you close the shell. Fix them before joining -- once the
shell is on you cannot reach inside.


----------------------------------------------------------------

5. JOINING AND FINISHING
----------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Fix the eyes to the head bump before you close the turtle.

  2. Hold the shell dome against the underside, wrong sides together, with the
head bump pointing away from the shell's 30 cm tail.

  3. Whip stitch the join using the shell's 30 cm tail. Match 24 to 24, not
24 to 35. The underside's outer round has 35 stitches, but they sit in only 24
anchor positions -- 19 plain stitches plus the 5 clusters. Put one shell stitch
against each anchor position, and where you come to a bump, pass the needle
through the base stitch the bump was worked into, not through the bump's own
stitches. The bumps will emerge from the join as the head and flippers.

  4. Add a small pinch of stuffing when you are about three quarters of the
way round. Do not overstuff -- a bag charm should stay flat enough to sit against
a bag, and the shell only holds about 8 cm³.

  5. Weave in every end inside the shell.

  6. Attach the keyring through two stitches at the back of the shell,
opposite the head, and knot the yarn inside. Going through two stitches rather
than one stops the ring from tearing out of the fabric with everyday use -- this
is the single most common failure on sold charms.


----------------------------------------------------------------

6. WHY THIS ONE SELLS AT MARKETS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Under 30 minutes, a couple of grams of yarn, and it hits three separate buying
motives at once: keyring, bag charm and pocket toy.

Make them in batches of one color, not one at a time. Six shells, then six
undersides, then six joins is roughly twice as fast as six complete turtles.

Price them as a small impulse buy and display them in a bowl at eye level rather
than flat on the table. Offer a mixed-color multi-buy -- most market sales are
two or three at once, not one.

State the real size in your listing. Shelby is about 3.7 cm across the
flippers. If you want the bigger charm, make the version below and list that one
instead -- do not list the small one as 6 cm.


----------------------------------------------------------------

MAKING A BIGGER SHELBY (ABOUT 5.7 CM ACROSS THE FLIPPERS)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Same pattern, worked to 42 stitches instead of 24. This gives a charm about
5.7 cm across the flippers, 5.2 cm long and 2.9 cm deep -- allow 45-50
minutes and about 4 g of yarn.

Shell. Rnd 1: 6 sc in MR (6). Rnd 2: inc in each st around (12). Rnd 3:
[sc, inc] x6 (18). Rnd 4: [2 sc, inc] x6 (24). Rnd 5: [3 sc, inc] x6 (30).
Rnd 6: [4 sc, inc] x6 (36). Rnd 7: [5 sc, inc] x6 (42). Rnd 8: BLO sc around
(42). Rnd 9: sc in each st around (42). FO, 30 cm tail.

Underside. Rnd 1 to Rnd 7 exactly as the shell's Rnd 1 to Rnd 7, ending at
42 stitches. Do not fasten off.

Head and flippers.

RND    INSTRUCTION                                                                                                                                                          STS   NOTE
-----  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------  ----  -----------------
Rnd 8  7 sc, (sc, hdc, hdc, sc) in next st, 8 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st, 7 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st, 8 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st, 7 sc, (sc, hdc, sc) in next st  (53)  head + 4 flippers

This consumes 7 + 1 + 8 + 1 + 7 + 1 + 8 + 1 + 7 + 1 = 42 stitches, exactly
closing the disc, and produces 53. The bumps land on stitches 8, 17, 25, 34 and
42, which spaces them 9, 8, 9, 8, 8 apart -- closer to even than the small
version. Join 42 to 42 by the same rule as step 3 above.


----------------------------------------------------------------

TROUBLESHOOTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Flippers are uneven. Count the plain stitches between the bumps: 3, 4, 3, 4,
3, then 2 at the end.

Keyring pulls out. Attach it through two stitches, not one, and knot the
yarn inside the shell.

Too fat to hang flat. Less stuffing. A charm needs just enough to hold the
dome.

Shell and base do not match. Both must finish at 24 stitches. Check Rnd 4 on
each piece.

The join will not pair up. You are counting the underside's 35 stitches.
Count anchor positions instead: 19 plain stitches plus 5 bumps = 24.

Head is floppy. The head bump is the 4-stitch one. Work it into a single
stitch so it stands proud.


----------------------------------------------------------------

THE FINISHED PROJECT
----------------------------------------------------------------

Shelby on a keyring -- about 3.7 cm across the flippers.

Before you close the shell, check three things: the eyes are fixed and
pull-tested, the five bumps are all present and the head is the four-stitch one,
and the shell's rim and the underside's Rnd 4 both read 24 stitches.

PATTERN 10 - DESIGN CODE NS 10 - WILLOW THE BUNNY LOVEY
================================================================
US crochet terms - Advanced beginner - 2-3 hours

A bunny comforter: a soft stuffed head above a light, drapey blanket body. Baby
items are the single strongest category on Etsy, and a lovey carries a far higher
price than a plain toy because it reads as a keepsake.

Willow's blanket is about 26 cm (10 in) square, her head is about 4.9 cm
(2 in) across, and she measures roughly 33 cm (13 in) from the ear tips to
the opposite bottom corner.


----------------------------------------------------------------

SAFETY -- READ THIS FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------------

Willow has no safety eyes, no buttons, no beads and no removable parts, and
her face is embroidered with floss knotted inside the head. That makes her face
safer than a safety-eyed toy.

It does not make her a tested toy. Willow has not been tested to a toy-safety
standard such as ASTM F963 or EN 71, and no homemade lovey should be described as
"baby-safe" without that testing.

Comforters are not recommended in the cot for babies under 12 months and
should be used with supervision. Put that note in your listing -- it is the correct
guidance, and buyers look for it.

Every seam on Willow should be sewn twice, and every end woven in for at least
5 cm and trimmed close. Loose ends are a genuine hazard on a baby item, not just
an untidy finish.


----------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Yarn -- DK (#3) 100% cotton, or a cotton blend, about 60 g. Cream, sage, dusty pink or pale grey.

Hook -- 3.5 mm (US E/4) for the blanket. Use 3.0 mm for the head if your
tension is loose; the head needs to be tight enough that stuffing cannot show.

Eyes -- None. The face is embroidered.

Floss -- Dark brown or charcoal cotton floss for the face. Knot every end
inside the head.

Also needed -- Polyester fiber fill, about 8 g; tapestry needle; stitch marker.

Only the head is stuffed. The blanket stays flat and drapey, which is the point.


----------------------------------------------------------------

GAUGE
----------------------------------------------------------------

DK cotton on a 3.5 mm hook: about 4.3 mm per stitch and 3.8 mm per round.

Check it on the blanket after Rnd 3. Count the double crochets along one edge:
three rounds gives 9 dc, which should measure about 39 mm. If your stitches are
narrower than 4.3 mm the blanket will come out small -- every size figure in this
pattern depends on that number, and it is worth 2 cm on the finished square.


----------------------------------------------------------------

FINISHED SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Blanket, 20 rounds           60 dc x 4.3 mm = about 26 cm square
---------------------------  -----------------------------------------------------
Head                         36 sts x 4.3 mm = 155 mm around = about 4.9 cm across
Head height                  14 rnd x 3.8 mm = about 5.3 cm
Ear tips to opposite corner  about 33 cm

The blanket grows about 1.3 cm per round on every side, so you can size it:

rounds  blanket
------  -----------
18      about 23 cm
20      about 26 cm
22      about 28 cm


----------------------------------------------------------------

ABBREVIATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

MR     magic ring
-----  -----------------------------------------
ch     chain
sc     single crochet
dc     double crochet
inc    increase -- 2 sc in one stitch
dec    invisible decrease
sl st  slip stitch
FO     fasten off
(n)    the stitch count at the end of that round


----------------------------------------------------------------

WORKING NOTES
----------------------------------------------------------------

The head and ears are worked in a continuous spiral -- do not join and do not
chain 1 between rounds. Mark the first stitch of every spiral round.

The blanket is worked in joined rounds: each round closes with a slip
stitch and the next begins with a turning chain. This is a different rhythm from
the spiral, and it is what lets the square grow outward evenly.

Unless a note says otherwise, work every stitch through both loops.


----------------------------------------------------------------

HOW IT COMES TOGETHER
----------------------------------------------------------------

The core techniques, in the order you meet them.

1 - The magic ring. The head and each ear begin with a magic ring. Pull the
tail tight once the first round is complete.

2 - Work in a spiral. Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round.
There is no join to count from.

3 - The granny-square corner. Every blanket corner is worked as (3 dc, ch 2,
3 dc) into one corner space. Those four corners are what make the piece square
instead of round. Miss one corner group on one round and the whole square pulls
out of true -- check all four at the end of every round.

4 - The border round. One round of single crochet around the finished edge,
with 3 sc into each corner space. It squares the edge off and stops the blanket
curling. Do not skip it.


----------------------------------------------------------------

1 - HEAD
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked as a slightly flattened sphere so it lies comfortably against a cot
mattress.

RND  INSTRUCTION            STS   NOTE
---  ---------------------  ----  --------------------
R1   6 sc in MR             (6)
R2   inc in each st around  (12)
R3   [sc, inc] x6           (18)
R4   [2 sc, inc] x6         (24)  ears attach at R4-R6
R5   [3 sc, inc] x6         (30)
R6   [4 sc, inc] x6         (36)  head at full width
R7   sc in each st around   (36)
R8   sc in each st around   (36)  face on R8-R10
R9   sc in each st around   (36)
R10  [4 sc, dec] x6         (30)
R11  [3 sc, dec] x6         (24)  stuff firmly
R12  [2 sc, dec] x6         (18)
R13  [sc, dec] x6           (12)  top up the stuffing
R14  dec x6                 (6)

Finish. FO and close with the tail. The finished head is about 4.9 cm across
and 5.3 cm tall.

Stuff the head firmly. A soft head on a lovey collapses the moment a baby
grips it, and the embroidered face distorts with it. Firm is correct here even
though the blanket is soft.


----------------------------------------------------------------

2 - EARS -- MAKE 2
----------------------------------------------------------------

Long and floppy -- the part a baby actually holds.

RND  INSTRUCTION           STS
---  --------------------  ----
R1   6 sc in MR            (6)
R2   [sc, inc] x3          (9)
R3   [2 sc, inc] x3        (12)
R4   sc in each st around  (12)
R5   sc in each st around  (12)
R6   sc in each st around  (12)
R7   sc in each st around  (12)
R8   sc in each st around  (12)
R9   [2 sc, dec] x3        (9)
R10  sc in each st around  (9)
R11  [sc, dec] x3          (6)

Finish. Do not stuff. Flatten. FO with a long tail.

Sew the ears to Rnds 4-6 of the head, about 10 stitches apart, and pinch
the base of each so it flops forward. Unstuffed ears are safer and far more
pleasant to hold.


----------------------------------------------------------------

3 - BLANKET BODY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Worked in joined rounds from the center out, as a four-corner granny-style
square in plain double crochet.

Ch 4 and sl st to the first ch to form a ring.

Rnd 1. Ch 3 (counts as the first dc), 2 dc into the ring, [ch 2, 3 dc into the
ring] x3, ch 2, sl st to the top of the ch 3.
[12 dc, 4 corner spaces]

Rnd 2. Sl st into the next corner space. Ch 3, 2 dc in the same space, ch 2,
3 dc in the same space -- this makes the first corner. Then into each remaining
corner space work (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Sl st to close.
[24 dc, 4 corners]

Rnds 3-20. Continue in the same way: into every corner space work
(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc), and into every space along a side work 3 dc.

Each round adds one 3-dc group to every side, so the count grows by 12 dc per
round:

RND  TOTAL DC
---  --------
3    36
5    60
10   120
15   180
20   240

At Rnd 20 that is 60 dc along each edge, which at 4.3 mm per dc gives a square
about 26 cm across -- the standard lovey size.

    Count your four corners at the end of every round. A round with only three
    (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) groups is the most common mistake in a granny square, and it
    will not show up until the blanket is half finished.

Border

Work one final round of sc all the way around: 1 sc into each dc and 3 sc into
each corner space. Sl st to close and FO.

At Rnd 20 that is 240 sc plus 12 into the corners = 252 sc.

This firms up the edge and stops the square from curling.


----------------------------------------------------------------

4 - FACE AND ASSEMBLY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Face

Embroider only -- no safety eyes, no beads, no buttons.

With dark brown floss, work two small oval eyes about 8 stitches apart across
Rnds 8-9, a small inverted-triangle nose centered between and below them, and
a shallow Y for the mouth.

Knot every end inside the head and bury the tails before you close it. On a
baby item a tail that works loose is not a small problem.

Blush

Skip it. Pastel and chalk blush rubs off onto bedding and is not washable.

Joining

Center the head over one corner of the square, overlapping the blanket by about
3 cm. Sew all the way around the base of the head, then sew it a second time.

This is the only structural seam on the piece, and it will be pulled, chewed and
washed. A single pass is not enough.

Finishing

Weave in every end for at least 5 cm and trim close.


----------------------------------------------------------------

5 - WHAT TO PUT IN YOUR LISTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Materials. 100% cotton, embroidered face, no safety eyes, no buttons, no
beads, no removable parts.

Care. Machine wash cool on a gentle cycle inside a mesh bag. Reshape while
damp and dry flat. Do not tumble dry -- it will felt and shrink the blanket.

Supervision. Comforters are not recommended in the cot for babies under
12 months and should be used with supervision.

Stating all three plainly raises conversion on baby listings. Buyers search for
"no safety eyes" and "machine washable" -- but do not claim "baby-safe" as a test
result you have not had. "Embroidered face, no small parts" says the same thing to
a buyer and is a claim you can stand behind.


----------------------------------------------------------------

TROUBLESHOOTING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Blanket curls at the edges -- You skipped the final sc border round. It is what
squares the edge off.

Square is not square -- Every corner needs (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Missing a corner
group on one round pulls the whole square out of true, and it compounds every
round after.

Blanket came out too small -- Your gauge is tighter than 4.3 mm per dc. Count
9 dc along an edge after Rnd 3: if they measure under 39 mm, go up a hook size or
add rounds.

Head flops over -- Stuff it firmly and sew the base seam twice. The head must
sit up off the blanket.

Face looks harsh -- Use fewer strands of floss and keep the eyes small and well
spaced.

Too small for a lovey -- Add rounds. Each extra round adds about 1.3 cm to each
side, so 22 rounds gives a 28 cm comforter.
