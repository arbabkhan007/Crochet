# DESIGN CODE NS 04

# Coco the Capybara

US crochet terms · Advanced beginner–intermediate · 2.5–3 hours

> **CHANGES FROM THE ORIGINAL — every one verified in `check/`**

> Unchanged: all 36 stitch counts (already correct), the spiral construction, the
> shallow R10 waist, and the overall shape.

A low, round, bottom-heavy capybara with a blunt sewn-on muzzle, plump stuffed legs and a calm embroidered sleeping face. No safety eyes — the face is embroidered.

> **About the skill level.** The rounds are all beginner-level. The one step that is
> not is the two-layer leg join at Rnd 4 and Rnd 5 — see technique 3. If you have
> never worked through two layers at once, make the legs first and practise the
> join on a scrap ring before you start the body.

---

## MATERIALS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Main yarn** | Worsted #4, warm brown, ~30 g. Smooth matte yarn shows the stitch texture best. |
| **Face yarn** | Worsted #4, dark brown, ~5 g. For the closed eyes, nose and mouth. Use the same weight as the main yarn — a lighter weight changes how the embroidery sits. |
| **Hook** | **3.0 mm (US C-2 or D-3).** Tight gauge so stuffing cannot show through. A 3.5 mm (US E/4) works only if you crochet tightly — see Troubleshooting. |
| **Eyes** | None — embroidered. The sleeping face is what gives Coco the calm expression. |
| **Other** | Polyfill **~5–8 g** (a handful — Coco holds far less than a bag holds), yarn needle, stitch marker, pins. Pins matter here: the muzzle and ears are pinned before sewing. |

> **Safety note.** Coco has no safety eyes and no small plastic parts. That makes
> the face safer than a safety-eyed toy, but it does **not** make the toy suitable
> for babies: it is a stuffed toy with a sewn-on muzzle and sewn-on ears, and it
> has not been tested to a toy-safety standard (ASTM F963 / EN 71). Intended as a
> decorative item or a gift for a child old enough not to chew it. Inspect the
> seams before giving it to a young child.

## GAUGE & FINISHED SIZE

**Gauge** — Worked at 3.0 mm and stuffed: **36 sc around measures about 52 mm in
diameter.** That is 4.5 mm per stitch and 4.3 mm per round. Check it on the body
after Rnd 6 rather than on a flat swatch — a stuffed tube is the only honest test
for amigurumi.

**Finished size** — About **10.3 cm (4 in) tall standing on all four legs** and
**5 cm (2 in) wide**. The body alone is 8.6 cm; the legs lift it a further
1.7 cm off the table. Coco is meant to be squat and bottom-heavy — the legs are
short relative to the round body, not long.

**Where that number comes from**

```
body, R1 to R20        20 rnd × 4.3 mm  =  86.0 mm
front legs, 9 rnd × 4.3 =  38.7 mm long, joining at R5 = 21.5 mm up
back  legs, 8 rnd × 4.3 =  34.4 mm long, joining at R4 = 17.2 mm up
=> each foot hangs      17.2 mm below the body  (38.7−21.5 = 34.4−17.2)
=> body floats           17.2 mm above the table
total standing height    86.0 + 17.2  = 103.2 mm  ≈ 10.3 cm
```

If you want a smaller Coco at about 8.5 cm, work the legs 5 rounds instead of 8
(≈ 21 mm) and join them at Rnd 1–2. Coco will then sit on her belly with the
feet tucked under, and section 3 no longer applies.

| **FO** | fasten off |
| **(n)** | stitch count at the end of the round |

### How to work this pattern

Work in **continuous spiral rounds** — do not join and do not ch 1 between
rounds. Move a stitch marker into the first stitch of every round; in a spiral
there is no join to count from.

## HOW IT COMES TOGETHER

The core techniques, in the order you will meet them.

**1 · THE MAGIC RING.** Every piece begins with a magic ring. Pull the tail tight
once the first round is complete.

**2 · WORK IN A SPIRAL.** Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every
round. There is no seam and no join to count from.

**3 · THE TWO-LAYER LEG JOIN.** *(photo recommended here)*
This is the only genuinely fiddly step in the pattern. You hold a finished leg
against the body and work one sc through **both** the leg and the body at the
same time — the leg edge and the body stitch are treated as a single stitch.
Those sc still count as one stitch each, so every stitch count in the body
table stays correct. Pinching the leg top flat first (see the Leg finishing
note) is what makes this easy; a round, un-pinched leg top will not lie flat
against the body and the join will pucker.

## 1. Legs, ears & muzzle — make these first

The legs are stuffed — plump little balls that the body rests on. Eight rounds
gives a 34 mm leg, which is what lets the feet reach the table from the low join
at Rnd 4–5.

### Leg — make 2 front legs (9 rounds) and 2 back legs (8 rounds)

| RND | INSTRUCTION | STS | WHO |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | 6 sc in MR | (6) | all |
| R2 | [1 sc, inc] ×3 | (9) | all |
| R3 | sc in each st around | (9) | all |
| R4 | sc in each st around | (9) | all |
| R5 | sc in each st around | (9) | all |
| R6 | sc in each st around | (9) | all |
| R7 | sc in each st around | (9) | all |
| R8 | sc in each st around | (9) | all — back legs stop here |
| R9 | sc in each st around | (9) | front legs only |

**Finish.** Back legs stop after R8 (8 rounds, ~34 mm). Front legs work R9 as
well (9 rounds, ~39 mm).

The extra round on the front legs is not decoration. The front legs join at
Rnd 5, which is 21.5 mm above the body's base; the back legs join at Rnd 4, only
17.2 mm up. A leg that starts higher has to be longer to reach the same floor:
9 × 4.3 = 38.7 mm minus 21.5 mm leaves the front foot 17.2 mm below the body,
and 8 × 4.3 = 34.4 mm minus 17.2 mm leaves the back foot 17.2 mm below it too.
Make all four legs the same length and the back feet land 8.6 mm lower — Coco
rocks back onto her haunches with her front feet in the air.

**Finish.** Stuff the lower half lightly. Then **pinch the whole 9-stitch top
flat into a narrow strip about 3 stitches wide** — press it between finger and
thumb and hold it that way while you work the next step. FO with a long tail.

> The leg top is 9 stitches around, so a flattened edge is naturally about 5
> stitches across. Pinching it down to 3 is deliberate: the stitches you are not
> joining bunch up inside the join, and that bunch is what makes the leg look
> plump instead of tubular. Do not try to keep the top flat and wide — it will
> not sit against the curved body.

### Ear — make 2

| RND | INSTRUCTION | STS |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | 6 sc in MR | (6) |
| R2 | [1 sc, inc] ×3 | (9) |
| R3 | sc in each st around | (9) |

**Finish.** FO, long tail. Do not stuff.

### Muzzle — make 1

| RND | INSTRUCTION | STS |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | 6 sc in MR | (6) |
| R2 | [1 sc, inc] ×3 | (9) |
| R3 | [2 sc, inc] ×3 | (12) |
| R4 | sc in each st around | (12) |
| R5 | sc in each st around | (12) |

**Finish.** FO, long tail. Stuff lightly.

## 2. Body & head — one piece, bottom up

Body and head are both 36 stitches around with only a shallow waist between
them, which is what makes Coco read as one continuous round blob rather than a
snowman. The legs are joined low, at Rnd 4 and Rnd 5, while the base is still
curving outward.

| RND | INSTRUCTION | STS | NOTE |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | 6 sc in MR | (6) | |
| R2 | inc in each st around | (12) | |
| R3 | [1 sc, inc] ×6 | (18) | |
| R4 | 3 sc, [1 sc, inc] ×3, 3 sc, [1 sc, inc] ×3 | (24) | BACK legs — see note |
| R5 | [3 sc, inc] ×6 | (30) | FRONT legs — see note |
| R6 | [4 sc, inc] ×6 | (36) | body at full width |
| R7 | sc in each st around | (36) | |
| R8 | sc in each st around | (36) | |
| R9 | sc in each st around | (36) | stuff body FIRMLY |
| R10 | [7 sc, invdec] ×4 | (32) | shallow waist |
| R11 | [7 sc, inc] ×4 | (36) | head begins |
| R12 | sc in each st around | (36) | |
| R13 | sc in each st around | (36) | |
| R14 | sc in each st around | (36) | eyes at R14–R15 |
| R15 | sc in each st around | (36) | |
| R16 | [4 sc, invdec] ×6 | (30) | |
| R17 | [3 sc, invdec] ×6 | (24) | stuff head firmly |
| R18 | [2 sc, invdec] ×6 | (18) | |
| R19 | [1 sc, invdec] ×6 | (12) | top up stuffing |
| R20 | invdec ×6 | (6) | |

**Finish.** Cinch closed, weave the tail inside.

### Joining the legs

Work R4 and R5 as the plain increase rounds printed above, but as you reach each
leg position, hold a pinched-flat leg top against the body and work the next
3 sc through **both** the leg and the body together (technique 3). Those 3 sc
still count as 3 stitches of the round, so the counts are unchanged.

**R4 — back legs.** Work the first 3 sc through a leg and the body, then
[1 sc, inc] ×3; work the next 3 sc through the second leg, then [1 sc, inc] ×3
to the end of the round **(24)**.

**R5 — front legs.** Work **7 sc**, join a leg over the next **3 sc**, work
**10 sc**, join a leg over the next **3 sc**, then work **7 sc** to the end of
the round **(30)**.

> **Why 7, 10 and 7.** R4 puts the two back legs opposite each other, as it has
> to — a 24-stitch round can only split two legs into 12 and 12. R5 is where you
> get a choice, and the front legs should **straddle** the back legs rather than
> line up behind them. This walk centres each front leg between the two back
> legs.
>
> Be clear about what this does and does not fix. It maximises the front-to-back
> stagger, which comes out at about **29 mm** — the most this construction
> allows. What it cannot do is make the footprint square: two joins on a
> 30-stitch round are always somewhere near opposite each other, so she stays
> about 65 mm across. The footprint ends up roughly **65 mm wide by 29 mm deep,
> about 2.2 : 1**, against the **6.75 : 1** you get if the front legs line up
> directly behind the back legs — that is a 9 mm deep footprint, which is why
> she tips.
>
> A genuinely square footprint needs all four legs on **one** round. Rnd 4 has
> 24 stitches, so four 3-stitch joins spaced 6 stitches apart gives a square
> about 40 mm × 40 mm. The catch is that Rnd 4 is only 17 mm up, so for the feet
> to reach the table the legs can be no longer than 17 mm — roughly 4 rounds.
> That works, and Coco still stands, but she becomes a shorter, squatter animal
> and section 3 stops applying. If Coco seems tippy front-to-back, that is the
> trade-off this design makes; widening her is the only real cure.

Hold the piece upside down and check all four legs sit square before you stuff.

## 3. Why the legs join so low

The back legs are 8 rounds (~34 mm) and join at Rnd 4, 17 mm above the body's
base. The front legs are 9 rounds (~39 mm) and join at Rnd 5, 21 mm up. Different
lengths, same result: every foot hangs **17.2 mm** below the body, so all four
land level and the underside of the body clears the table by about that much.
Coco stands on her feet, not on her belly.

Note what this costs. Because the legs really do reach the table, they lift the
whole body 17 mm — which is why the finished height is about 10.3 cm and not the
8.6 cm the body measures on its own.

This is the single most common failure on a round-bodied animal: legs joined at
the widest part of the body are always too high, the belly rests on the table
and the feet dangle in the air. If Coco does not stand, check the join height
before anything else.

## 4. Face & assembly

**Muzzle.** Stuff lightly and pin to the front of the head over **R12–R14**. Sew
all the way around with matching brown. It should sit proud of the head, not
flush. The muzzle is 17 mm across and the eyes go 27 mm apart, which leaves
about 5 mm of clear face between the muzzle edge and each eye — pin the muzzle
centred, or it will crowd one eye.

**Eyes.** Embroider, do not use safety eyes. With dark brown, work a shallow
downward arc about 3 stitches wide on each side at **R14–R15**, **6 stitches
apart**, with a tiny tick angled down at each outer end. Six stitches keeps both
eyes on the front of the face; wider and they wrap around the sides. Placing
them at R14–R15 rather than lower keeps the whole eye **above** the muzzle,
which is pinned over R12–R14. This closed-eye curve is what makes Coco look
asleep.

**Nose & mouth.** On the muzzle, embroider a small dark triangle at the top
centre, then a short vertical line down from it and a soft curved mouth to one
side. Embroider the nose **before** sewing the muzzle on only if you prefer a
flat surface to work on; otherwise sew first and embroider onto the curve. Pick
one and be consistent — the nose sits differently each way.

**Ears.** Pinch the base of each ear so it cups forward, then sew at **R15**,
about **5 stitches apart**, angled slightly outward. R15 is still a full 36-stitch
round, so 5 stitches puts the ears on top of the head where a capybara's ears
belong. Sewing them 7 stitches apart — or onto R16, which has already decreased
to 30 stitches — pushes them out to the sides of the head and they disappear
from the front.

**Legs.** Already joined during R4 and R5 — no sewing needed. Just make sure the
pinched 3-stitch strip of each leg is caught fully in the round.

**Shaping.** Roll the finished piece gently between your palms to settle the
stuffing into a round, bottom-heavy shape.

## TROUBLESHOOTING

**Coco looks too tall** — Almost always too many plain rounds. **R12–R15 is the
straight section of the head and the face is positioned on it;** adding "just one
more" round each time turns the shape into a tower and moves the face off the
round it was designed for.

**Will not stand** — Check the join height, not the spacing. The legs belong on
R4 and R5. Joined any higher, the legs cannot clear the body's underside.

**Tips forward or backward** — Two causes, in order of likelihood. First, the
front legs are lined up behind the back legs instead of straddling them: on R5
the walk must be **7 sc, 10 sc, 7 sc**. Second, Coco is narrow front-to-back by
design (about 29 mm) and no walk fixes that — if she still tips, see the note in
section 2 about moving all four legs to one round.

**Rocks back onto her haunches, front feet in the air** — All four legs are the
same length. They cannot be: the front legs join at Rnd 5, 21.5 mm up, and the
back legs at Rnd 4, 17.2 mm up, so equal-length legs put the back feet 8.6 mm
lower. Work the **front legs 9 rounds and the back legs 8**; both feet then hang
17.2 mm below the body and land level.

**Will not sit level** — Stuff the lower body firmly. A soft base lets Coco tip
forward onto the muzzle.

**Head flops back** — The waist at R10 is deliberately shallow (32 sts). Do not
decrease it further; a narrow neck cannot hold the head upright on this shape.

**Muzzle looks flat** — Stuff it just enough to hold a dome.

**Stuffing shows** — You are on too large a hook, or your tension is loose. The
pattern is written for **3.0 mm**. If you switched to 3.5 mm, go back down;
3.5 mm only works for tight crocheters.

**Legs have a small hole where they meet the body** — You joined a flat, wide leg
top instead of a pinched one. Flatten the whole 9-stitch top into a 3-stitch
strip before joining, so the loose stitches bunch inside the join.

**Colourways** — Classic warm brown, soft grey, sandy beige, and cocoa.
