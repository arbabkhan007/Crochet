# DESIGN CODE NS 10

# Willow the Bunny Lovey

**US crochet terms · Advanced beginner · 2–3 hours**

A bunny comforter: a soft stuffed head above a light, drapey blanket body. Baby
items are the single strongest category on Etsy, and a lovey carries a far higher
price than a plain toy because it reads as a keepsake.

Willow's blanket is about **26 cm (10 in) square**, her head is about **4.9 cm
(2 in)** across, and she measures roughly **33 cm (13 in)** from the ear tips to
the opposite bottom corner.

---

## SAFETY — READ THIS FIRST

Willow has **no safety eyes, no buttons, no beads and no removable parts**, and
her face is embroidered with floss knotted inside the head. That makes her face
safer than a safety-eyed toy.

**It does not make her a tested toy.** Willow has not been tested to a toy-safety
standard such as ASTM F963 or EN 71, and no homemade lovey should be described as
"baby-safe" without that testing.

Comforters are **not recommended in the cot for babies under 12 months** and
should be used with supervision. Put that note in your listing — it is the correct
guidance, and buyers look for it.

Every seam on Willow should be sewn twice, and every end woven in for at least
5 cm and trimmed close. Loose ends are a genuine hazard on a baby item, not just
an untidy finish.

---

## MATERIALS

**Yarn** — DK (#3) 100% cotton, or a cotton blend, about 60 g. Cream, sage, dusty pink or pale grey.

**Hook** — 3.5 mm (US E/4) for the blanket. Use 3.0 mm for the head if your
tension is loose; the head needs to be tight enough that stuffing cannot show.

**Eyes** — None. The face is embroidered.

**Floss** — Dark brown or charcoal cotton floss for the face. Knot every end
inside the head.

**Also needed** — Polyester fiber fill, about 8 g; tapestry needle; stitch marker.

Only the head is stuffed. The blanket stays flat and drapey, which is the point.

---

## GAUGE

DK cotton on a 3.5 mm hook: about **4.3 mm per stitch** and **3.8 mm per round**.

Check it on the blanket after Rnd 3. Count the double crochets along one edge:
three rounds gives 9 dc, which should measure about 39 mm. If your stitches are
narrower than 4.3 mm the blanket will come out small — every size figure in this
pattern depends on that number, and it is worth 2 cm on the finished square.

## WORKING NOTES

The **head and ears** are worked in a continuous spiral — do not join and do not
chain 1 between rounds. Mark the first stitch of every spiral round.

The **blanket** is worked in **joined rounds**: each round closes with a slip
stitch and the next begins with a turning chain. This is a different rhythm from
the spiral, and it is what lets the square grow outward evenly.

Unless a note says otherwise, work every stitch through both loops.

---

## HOW IT COMES TOGETHER

The core techniques, in the order you meet them.

**1 · The magic ring.** The head and each ear begin with a magic ring. Pull the
tail tight once the first round is complete.

**2 · Work in a spiral.** Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round.
There is no join to count from.

**3 · The granny-square corner.** Every blanket corner is worked as **(3 dc, ch 2,
3 dc) into one corner space**. Those four corners are what make the piece square
instead of round. Miss one corner group on one round and the whole square pulls
out of true — check all four at the end of every round.

**4 · The border round.** One round of single crochet around the finished edge,
with 3 sc into each corner space. It squares the edge off and stops the blanket
curling. Do not skip it.

Worked as a slightly flattened sphere so it lies comfortably against a cot
mattress.

**Stuff the head firmly.** A soft head on a lovey collapses the moment a baby
grips it, and the embroidered face distorts with it. Firm is correct here even
though the blanket is soft.

Long and floppy — the part a baby actually holds.

|---|---|---|
| R1 | 6 sc in MR | (6) |
| R2 | [sc, inc] ×3 | (9) |
| R3 | [2 sc, inc] ×3 | (12) |
| R4 | sc in each st around | (12) |
| R5 | sc in each st around | (12) |
| R6 | sc in each st around | (12) |
| R7 | sc in each st around | (12) |
| R8 | sc in each st around | (12) |
| R9 | [2 sc, dec] ×3 | (9) |
| R10 | sc in each st around | (9) |
| R11 | [sc, dec] ×3 | (6) |

**Finish.** Do not stuff. Flatten. FO with a long tail.

Sew the ears to **Rnds 4–6** of the head, about **10 stitches apart**, and pinch
the base of each so it flops forward. Unstuffed ears are safer and far more
pleasant to hold.

---

## 3 · BLANKET BODY

Worked in **joined rounds** from the center out, as a four-corner granny-style
square in plain double crochet.

**Ch 4 and sl st to the first ch to form a ring.**

**Rnd 1.** Ch 3 (counts as the first dc), 2 dc into the ring, [ch 2, 3 dc into the
ring] ×3, ch 2, sl st to the top of the ch 3.
**[12 dc, 4 corner spaces]**

**Rnd 2.** Sl st into the next corner space. Ch 3, 2 dc in the same space, ch 2,
3 dc in the same space — this makes the first corner. Then into each remaining
corner space work (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Sl st to close.
**[24 dc, 4 corners]**

**Rnds 3–20.** Continue in the same way: into every **corner space** work
(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc), and into every space **along a side** work 3 dc.

Each round adds one 3-dc group to every side, so the count grows by **12 dc per
round**:

| RND | TOTAL DC |
|---|---|
| 3 | 36 |
| 5 | 60 |
| 10 | 120 |
| 15 | 180 |
| 20 | 240 |

At Rnd 20 that is **60 dc along each edge**, which at 4.3 mm per dc gives a square
about 26 cm across — the standard lovey size.

> Count your four corners at the end of every round. A round with only three
> (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) groups is the most common mistake in a granny square, and it
> will not show up until the blanket is half finished.

### Border

Work one final round of sc all the way around: **1 sc into each dc and 3 sc into
each corner space**. Sl st to close and FO.

At Rnd 20 that is 240 sc plus 12 into the corners = **252 sc**.

This firms up the edge and stops the square from curling.

---

## 4 · FACE AND ASSEMBLY

### Face

Embroider only — no safety eyes, no beads, no buttons.

With dark brown floss, work two small oval eyes about **8 stitches apart** across
**Rnds 8–9**, a small inverted-triangle nose centered between and below them, and
a shallow Y for the mouth.

**Knot every end inside the head and bury the tails before you close it.** On a
baby item a tail that works loose is not a small problem.

### Blush

Skip it. Pastel and chalk blush rubs off onto bedding and is not washable.

### Joining

Center the head over one corner of the square, overlapping the blanket by about
3 cm. Sew all the way around the base of the head, **then sew it a second time**.

This is the only structural seam on the piece, and it will be pulled, chewed and
washed. A single pass is not enough.

### Finishing

Weave in every end for at least 5 cm and trim close.

---

## 5 · WHAT TO PUT IN YOUR LISTING

**Materials.** 100% cotton, embroidered face, no safety eyes, no buttons, no
beads, no removable parts.

**Care.** Machine wash cool on a gentle cycle inside a mesh bag. Reshape while
damp and dry flat. Do not tumble dry — it will felt and shrink the blanket.

**Supervision.** Comforters are not recommended in the cot for babies under
12 months and should be used with supervision.

Stating all three plainly raises conversion on baby listings. Buyers search for
"no safety eyes" and "machine washable" — but do not claim "baby-safe" as a test
result you have not had. "Embroidered face, no small parts" says the same thing to
a buyer and is a claim you can stand behind.

---

## TROUBLESHOOTING

**Blanket curls at the edges** — You skipped the final sc border round. It is what
squares the edge off.

**Square is not square** — Every corner needs (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Missing a corner
group on one round pulls the whole square out of true, and it compounds every
round after.

**Blanket came out too small** — Your gauge is tighter than 4.3 mm per dc. Count
9 dc along an edge after Rnd 3: if they measure under 39 mm, go up a hook size or
add rounds.

**Head flops over** — Stuff it firmly and sew the base seam twice. The head must
sit up off the blanket.

**Face looks harsh** — Use fewer strands of floss and keep the eyes small and well
spaced.

**Too small for a lovey** — Add rounds. Each extra round adds about 1.3 cm to each
side, so 22 rounds gives a 28 cm comforter.

single stitch.
